AI mind mappers and canvas tools

AI mind-map tools have converged on one pattern: a prompt, document, video or audio file goes in, a text-outline map comes out, and a chat panel or an expand button refines it. Image support is limited to single stickers on a topic. No verified product combines AI generation in a requested style, a per-subtree switch to generated raster or SVG art that stays connected and collapsible, an organic look, a fast native canvas, and use of an existing subscription through a command-line tool, and that combination is the gap Radiant targets.

Evidence

The research behind this page was capped in tool calls, so coverage is uneven. Most rankings of AI mind-map tools are written by vendors, and the main comparison source was written by the chief executive of one of the compared products. Treat the tool-by-tool material as a map of the market, not a verified benchmark. Dates are as of 17 November 2026.

How AI map tools work today

The common pattern is the same across nearly every product. The user supplies a prompt, a document, a YouTube link, an audio file or a web page. A language model produces a text outline. The app lays that outline out as a map using a generic editor theme. The user then refines the map through chat, an expand button, or a click on a node. Style control, where it exists, means choosing an editor theme, not describing a visual style in words.

Refinement styles

  • Chat bound to the live map. The chat can summarise, expand or reorganise a section of the map that is already on screen. Mapify and Xmind work this way, and Miro does so for prompts on its board. MindMap AI blog
  • One-click expand. MindMeister offers an "AI Expand" button on a node and has no chat. MindMap AI blog
  • Click a node to ask. In NotebookLM the user clicks a node and the side chat answers about it. Google support
  • Regenerate from scratch. EdrawMind made a new map on every chat turn and kept one file per chat. Mymap.ai creates a new map for each follow-up. Monica is grouped with these by the notes. MindMap AI blog
  • Chat that cannot see the map. GitMind's chat does not see the map. Whimsical's chat edits content but fails at structural changes such as moving nodes, and a reviewer reported that Miro's layout broke after AI edits. MindMap AI blog

The inference from these reports is that refinement quality varies sharply. Tools whose chat is bound to the live map keep the user's edits, while tools that regenerate throw them away. A branch-scoped regenerate or expand command is the minimum expectation. The Whimsical and Miro reports also suggest that the layout engine should stay authoritative and the model should edit the tree, not coordinates.

Risk

The comparison that supplies most of the refinement findings was written by the chief executive of MindMap AI, who ranks his own product first. The tests were done in July 2026, cannot be reproduced, and the article contains internal price inconsistencies. The refinement pattern is plausible and matches other reviews, but individual verdicts on competitors should be read as marketing.

Independent reviewers describe the market in role terms: Mapify for turning documents into maps, Xmind for polished editable output, Whimsical for fast prompt drafts, and Miro for team workshops. They name no consensus winner. AFFiNE guide Scholarly

Mapify

Web, mobile, Chrome extension

Takes PDF, YouTube, audio, documents and images. Chat can summarise, expand or reorganise a section of the same map. File input and PDF, SVG and Markdown export are paid. Free gives 30 one-time credits. MindMap AI blog

Xmind AI

Desktop and mobile; full profile on the desktop tools page

Xmind Agent is described as the centrepiece of "Xmind 2027". It creates maps from ideas or source material, answers questions about a map and edits or reorganises branches. It is included on all plans and spends AI credits. An independent source for a "2027" release was not found, so treat the name as a vendor and Product Hunt item. Product Hunt Xmind pricing

On the official page, text-to-map, summarise, refine, task breakdown, pitch video and image generation sit on Premium, with 500 AI credits a month. A May 2026 newsletter adds image-to-editable-map. Xmind newsletter

Miro

Web, team workshops

Generates a mind map, flowchart, ER diagram or UML diagram from a prompt onto the board, where it stays live for comments. A reviewer saw layout break after AI edits. Miro announced new AI platform items on 19 May 2026; the notes did not verify the details. Miro Miro newsroom

MindMeister

Online only

An AI draft plus an "AI Expand" button on a node. No chat. It is the cleanest example of branch-scoped one-click AI. MindMap AI blog

NotebookLM

Google, launched around March 2025

A "Mind Map" chip in the chat builds a map from the notebook's sources in about 20 seconds. The map zooms, pans, expands and collapses. Clicking a node asks the chat about it. It downloads as an image, a regenerate means deleting the note and redoing it, it is not in the mobile app, and it can be inaccurate. Google support 9to5Google

Images and illustration

Nothing found works at subtree level. The closest shipping feature is Xmind's "Make Image", which acts on one topic at a time.

Xmind Make Image

Xmind announced the feature in a blog post on 11 March 2026, with release notes on 16 March 2026. For a selected topic, the AI reads the topic and the surrounding map, drafts an image prompt and suggests a style. Presets are 3D, Sketch and Oil Painting (with a Realistic variant), and a Custom Style option takes free text. An "Inspire" button re-rolls the prompt. The result is a "sticker": for presets, the background is removed automatically, and the image is inserted on the topic. The notes do not state plan gating, but the official pricing page lists image generation under Premium. Xmind blog Xmind guide Xmind pricing

The part worth copying is the prompt drafting from map context: the user does not write an image prompt, because the app derives one from where the topic sits in the map. The limit is scope. It produces one sticker per topic, with no style shared across a subtree and no connection between the art and the branch.

Other image experiments

  • MindMap AI picture nodes. Nodes can hold pictures, but the notes do not say whether the pictures are generated or how they relate to the style of the map. mindmapai.app
  • Single-image root experiment. An Agent.ai "Mindmap Generator" has the model write an indented text tree, a custom text-to-map service lays it out as SVG, and one DALL-E 3 image (zen, a single pastel colour, thick brushstrokes) is generated for the central concept. It is circle-clipped with an SVG clip-path and embedded as base64. There are no per-node images, and the layout is two-sided horizontal. DEV Community
  • tldraw image-pipeline starter kit. tldraw 4.4 (19 February 2026) ships an MIT-licensed starter kit for a node-based image-generation pipeline on an infinite canvas, plus an improved agent starter kit. It is a pipeline on a canvas, not a mind map. tldraw blog
  • Developer guides. Guides suggest prompting DALL-E with a structured mind-map description, or having GPT-4 write Mermaid and generating a separate illustration. Single-image "mind map" outputs also exist on stock-AI sites, with quality unverified. Medium guide Easy-Peasy
Idea for Radiant

Whole-image "illustrated mind map" outputs from image models are non-editable bitmaps, and text errors are a known image-model weakness (general knowledge, not sourced in the research). A hybrid avoids both: the language model writes the tree, a deterministic layout places it, per-node or per-subtree art is generated separately, and the app renders all text itself. No product found does this. This is a proposed design, not prior art.

Not verified

The statement that nothing works at subtree level rests on a limited search of product pages, reviews and developer posts. Image-model communities (Midjourney, Ideogram, Recraft) and code repositories were not searched for prototypes. Absence of evidence is not proof of absence.

Canvas tools worth studying

These tools are not AI mind mappers. They matter because their engineering and conventions are documented, and Radiant needs both a fast canvas and familiar canvas behaviour.

tldraw

Infinite canvas SDK

Level-of-detail rendering by zoom or on-screen size, and reactive state that tracks dependencies automatically. tldraw perf docs Version 4.4 moved shape indicators from SVG to a 2D canvas (up to 25 times faster in some cases), added an R-tree spatial index for brush, scribble and erase, and skips checks for off-screen shapes. tldraw blog No tldraw shape-count limit was retrieved. For scale, one developer's pixi.js canvas reportedly beat a DOM version about tenfold and broke near 60,000 cards (a single anecdote). DEV

Figma renderer

Post 18 September 2025

A C++ renderer compiled to WebAssembly and native, using Dawn for WebGPU, batching uniforms in one buffer, translating shaders from GLSL to WGSL with naga, and falling back from WebGPU to WebGL at runtime, including mid-session, with a device blocklist. Gains on some devices, no regressions. Figma blog

Excalidraw and rough.js

Open source

An infinite canvas with an open .excalidraw JSON file and a hand-drawn look credited to rough.js. rough.js is under 9 kB, draws on canvas or SVG, and offers roughness, bowing and fill styles such as hachure and cross-hatch. rough README Excalidraw repo

perfect-freehand

Stroke library

getStroke(points) returns an outline polygon with simulated pressure, taper at start and end, thinning and smoothing; the caller renders it as an SVG path or on canvas. Output may self-intersect. It is a ready reference for tapered organic branches, though it is not a mind-map renderer. perfect-freehand

Heptabase

Cards on whiteboards

Zooming out keeps section titles legible. This is the only semantic-zoom precedent found: the view changes what it shows by zoom level, not only the size. Heptabase recommends a higher-level whiteboard above about 100 cards, and its October 2026 release 1.112.8 improved dragging, loading and rendering of large whiteboards. Heptabase sections Heptabase changelog

Kinopio

Cards and connections

Space or middle mouse pans, command or control with scroll zooms, and Z toggles full zoom-out and zoom-in. It has a minimap and table of contents, though a later release says "Positional Zoom" replaces minimaps. One user reports that moving connected cards while zoomed out can mess up connections. Kinopio help PKM Diaries

Markmap

Markdown to map

Turns Markdown headings and lists into a map. It has an SVG renderer and a canvas renderer for very large maps, tree, two-sided and org-chart layouts, and a command-line tool that exports standalone HTML, SVG or PNG. Layout is automatic only. markmap README

Obsidian Canvas and JSON Canvas

Open file format

Canvas stores open JSON Canvas files. Arrows are reportedly not visible to backlinks or the graph. Kinopio also imports and exports the format. Zeli Kinopio help

Large-board engines

Atlassian, MURAL

Atlassian's Confluence Whiteboards uses a custom WebGL renderer, targeting 5,000 elements with 50 collaborators. MURAL moved from a DOM-heavy design to a custom canvas engine and reports an 80 per cent improvement in the share of frames at 30 frames per second or higher (a vendor figure). Atlassian MURAL on DEV

For a native app the inference is that a spatial index, level of detail and a GPU renderer with cached rasters per subtree follow directly from tldraw and Figma practice. Image-heavy maps would additionally need texture atlases or mip levels. That second point is inference, not sourced.

Formats and reliability for AI output

Not verified

The evidence here is thin. No head-to-head test of which format language models generate most reliably for mind maps was found, and the items below come from single projects and general benchmarks.

  • A Humboldt University Berlin project on LLM-drawn diagrams raised Mermaid validity from about 70 per cent to near 100 per cent with a render, check and repair loop plus a syntax reference in the prompt. It also used a two-stage approach: the model writes JSON, and deterministic code converts it. This is a single project. HU Berlin
  • Microsoft GenAIScript reports that models sometimes emit invalid Mermaid but can repair it when given the parser error. GenAIScript
  • Aider benchmarks found models make more errors when code is wrapped in JSON, because of quoting and escaping. Aider
  • A DEV estimate puts XML or JSON at up to 24 times the tokens of Mermaid for a sequence diagram. This is an estimate. DEV
  • The StructEval benchmark covers JSON, Markdown and Mermaid-type outputs, but per-format numbers were not retrieved. alphaXiv
  • The Agent.ai generator found that including an example in the prompt reduced malformed trees. DEV Community

The inference that follows is a design recommendation, not a finding. Ask the model for an outline first, either indented Markdown or compact JSON checked against a schema, and compile it deterministically into the map. An indented outline streams line by line, so nodes can appear as they are generated, while JSON needs incremental parsing. Queue per-node image or SVG jobs after the tree exists. The notes found no source on streaming onto a canvas in existing tools. Raw Mermaid is the format most likely to need repair loops.

What this means for Radiant

Implication for Radiant

The market validates the outline-in, map-out pipeline and shows which refinement commands users get: chat on the live map, node-scoped ask, one-click expand. These are table stakes, and Radiant should implement them as branch-scoped edits. The differentiator is subtree art, and the nearest precedent (Xmind Make Image) shows the useful parts: context-derived image prompts, style presets, a custom style, and background removal.

  • Treat the layout engine as authoritative. The model edits a validated outline, and deterministic code positions and renders it. Reports of broken layout after AI edits in Miro and failed structural edits in Whimsical support this.
  • Define a documented, open map file (JSON or Markdown outline) so that coding agents can read and write Radiant maps. Xmind, Heptabase and the JSON Canvas ecosystem show that this is how agent integration is done.
  • Generate per-subtree art as a queued job after the tree exists, with a shared style, and keep text rendered by the app.
  • Use a spatial index, level of detail and cached rasters per subtree for the canvas, and show subtree titles at far zoom, following Heptabase.
  • Premium-gated AI with credits is what the command-line subscription design avoids. Xmind's Premium tier gives 500 credits a month; Radiant would draw on the user's own Codex login instead.

See the AI engine page, the art subtrees page and the engine page for how these become requirements.

Limits of this research

  • Tools never individually researched: Taskade (beyond a keyboard request), Mindomo, Albus, Heuristica, FigJam, Lucidspark, Milanote beyond a rating, Scrintal, Muse, Apple Freeform, Logseq whiteboards, AFFiNE Edgeless, and Obsidian AI mind-map plugins. Coggle has no AI mentioned in the sources found. No 2025 to 2026 native Mac newcomer was identified.
  • Most rankings and the main refinement comparison are vendor-written. No G2, Reddit or Hacker News sentiment on map quality was checked.
  • Pricing conflicts. Xmind: an earlier competitor blog gave $10 a month or $59.04 a year, while the official page shows $4.92 for Pro and $8.25 for Premium, apparently billed yearly but not stated. Ayoa: a competitor blog cites $13 per user a month annually, an earlier pass said about $10, and the official page lists no prices. Official figures are preferred where they exist.
  • Kosmik: one source says it was sunset on 31 May 2026, while others still list pricing. The date is unverified. Toolradar
  • Whether text follows a branch curve, and how Ayoa renders its branches, were not confirmed from any source. A GetApp listing says branch thickness cannot be adjusted in Ayoa, which contradicts the vendor claim. GetApp
  • Image-model communities and code repositories were not searched for prototypes of per-node or per-subtree generated art.
  • Several pages returned only partial content, so some items are at search-summary level. Miro's AI quality rests on one reviewer, and Miro's May 2026 announcements were not detailed.
