A blueprint for a mind map app that helps you remember

This report covers what Tony Buzan taught, what the evidence supports, what existing products do, what people ask for, and what to build: a fast desktop app in which an AI drafts the map from a topic and any branch can become pictures or SVG artwork. It ends with a stack recommendation backed by measurements taken on your own Mac.

The short version

Nobody has built this

Every AI mind-map tool found produces a text outline map. The closest thing to your idea is a feature in Xmind that generates one sticker for one topic. No product was found that turns a whole subtree into generated pictures or SVG artwork while keeping it connected and collapsible. AI and canvas tools

The building blocks work today

On your installed Codex CLI, with your existing login, a map streamed in at about one node every 0.3 seconds, a picture came back in about 12 seconds, a transparent background worked, and a generated SVG passed every check of the proposed contract. Each was a single test. Codex CLI

A WebView is fast enough

A test on your M4 Pro held 120 frames per second with 100,000 tapered branches in WebGL2. The catch is that a default system WebView is capped at 60; reaching 120 needs one private setting. Stack decision

Recommended stack

Tauri with a Rust host, a TypeScript front end and a small custom WebGL2 renderer. The library you asked about, nanoui, cannot draw curved tapered branches today. Elixir is not needed in version 1. Zig fits later as small WebAssembly kernels. Stack decision

Making beats viewing

The strongest evidence is that building or reworking a map helps memory more than studying a finished one, and that self-testing and spaced review help most of all. So the AI should produce a draft to rework, and the app should include recall and review. The evidence

Pictures must carry meaning

Pictures help when they represent the idea and can hurt when they are decoration. Distinctiveness only works by contrast. That is why artwork is something you choose for particular subtrees, and why it can be switched off. Picture and SVG subtrees

Buzan, checked against his own books

His ten laws were read in his 2018 text, with his reason for each. Two famous lines attributed to him do not appear in either book that was read, and his one-word-per-branch rule has no research behind it. His laws become defaults in Radiant, not rules. Buzan's laws

What users want fixed

Layouts that reshuffle on their own, weak keyboard flow, proprietary files, subscriptions, and maps that are trees when thought is a graph. Reddit could not be read, so its communities are missing from the picture. Community voice

Frame rate120 on high-refresh displays through one private WebKit setting, with automatic fallback to 60.Acceptable for a personal app distributed directly; Electron to be measured before any public release.
CanvasA small custom WebGL2 renderer; WebGPU later.A handful of batched draw calls held 100,000 branches at 120 frames per second.
Engine corePure TypeScript modules for model, layout and geometry, portable to WebAssembly.Fastest to build; hot spots can move to Rust or Zig if profiling demands.
AI engineOne long-lived codex app-server process behind a provider interface.It streams, cancels and reports limits; the interface allows other engines later.
Map generationThe model writes one node per line; the app validates each line and does all layout.Lines can be applied as they arrive, so the map grows while it generates.
RepresentationsThe tree is the truth; vector, picture and SVG are views of it that can be switched back at any time.Search, outline, export and accessibility keep working, and nothing is lost by trying artwork.
PicturesOne image per node from a shared style sheet; the app draws all labels and branches.Image models still make errors in text, and consistency needs a shared style.

How this report was made

Seventeen research passes ran in two rounds. The first round covered Buzan and history, the learning science, MindNode and desktop products, AI and canvas tools, community discussion, the Codex CLI, Zig, other stacks, and canvas engine design. The second round closed the gaps the first left: Buzan's rules were checked against the text of two of his books, the remaining learning research was collected, the nanoui source was read file by file, native stacks were surveyed, and the two hands-on tests were run. Twelve writers then turned the notes into these pages, working from a shared set of decisions.

Not verified

Several things could not be reached and are missing or thin as a result. Reddit refused automated access and was not read. Journal publishers blocked full texts, so most effect sizes come from abstracts and should be checked before being quoted. The Mind Map Book could not be read directly, so its laws are reported from secondary summaries. Competitor prices come partly from aggregators that disagree. Each research page ends with its own list of limits.

The full research notes, with a source link for every claim, are in the research_notes folder of this project. Every external source cited in the report is listed on the sources page.
