Radiant · design study

Branches with depth

Eleven ways to make the branches of a mind map three-dimensional. Every stage below draws the same 40-node map live in WebGL. Move the pointer over a stage to tilt it, and press Replay growth to watch the map draw itself from the root.

A · Shade the existing strokes

A look, not a scene

Radiant already turns each branch into a ribbon of triangles. Tell the shader how far across the ribbon each pixel is, treat that as the curve of a cylinder, and light it. Layout, text, hit-testing and export stay as they are.

B · Real geometry, fixed camera

Shallow depth

Branches become actual meshes a few millimetres tall, seen by a camera that is fixed or only slightly tilted. Needed when things must pass over each other or cast a shadow that is not directly beneath them.

C · Separate 3D scene

A place you look around

A different layout in real space with a camera that orbits. Spectacular, and a different product surface: best treated as a presentation mode.

Side by side

Where I would start

  1. Soft clay first, on route A. It keeps the colours and the legibility of today's maps, and building it produces the machinery (a normal across the stroke, one light, a soft shadow) that bark, neon, kintsugi and emboss all reuse.
  2. Living bark as the showpiece theme. It is your original idea, it is the one people will screenshot, and on route A it costs one texture set more than clay.
  3. Neon or kintsugi for dark themes. Both are cheap once clay exists.
  4. Lifted arcs if you want real depth while the text stays flat and editable.
  5. Bonsai later, as a presentation mode. It needs its own layout and should not be where people edit.

Questions for you

  1. Skin or space? Should 3D be a look on the flat map you edit (routes A and B), or do you also want to move around the map in 3D (route C)?
  2. Must branch colours survive? That decides whether bark, kintsugi and emboss are in or out.
  3. Where does it live? One more branch style in a theme, a separate "material" setting, or something you can set per branch?
  4. How much motion? Growth, flow and pulses all the time, only while AI is generating, or only when presenting?
  5. What must stay smooth? The largest map and the weakest Mac it has to run well on.
  6. Which two or three do I take further? I can build the first one into Radiant behind a theme.
