# Fundamental analysis of a stock {#13sQgiw6MiIi sticker=📈}

Goal: estimate what a business is worth and compare that with its price. Work through the nine branches in order: understand, read, measure, value, decide.

## 1 Understand the business {#business sticker=🏢 side=right}

If you cannot explain how the company earns money in two sentences, stop here.

- What it sells {#77F3Vw9eXCKC}
  - Products and segments {#KJmOOMEukawI}
  - Revenue by segment {#LYjzaJaHALFM}
  - Recurring vs one-time sales {#XnOI6W1brR4u}
- How it makes money {#model}
  - Price × volume drivers {#rJ8LTXSA6DiW}
  - Unit economics {#bENNaiUkf4xv}
    Profit per customer or per unit: lifetime value against the cost to win the customer.
  - Fixed vs variable costs {#mI3oKfdRPmqg}
- Customers {#aYCOxljxCk0N}
  - Who buys and why {#ayIvJpJDRUek}
  - Customer concentration {#QJLPX3zWrUOx}
    One customer above 10% of sales is a risk worth naming.
  - Retention and churn {#KJfN5Hal3Jgy}
- Industry {#industry}
  - Market size and growth {#9LgByfi5JGiK}
  - Cyclical or steady {#g9PHmqG8IVpf}
  - Rivals and market share {#wuxUwE60ZUDc}
  - Regulation {#QTtOLQ9msoFi}
- Competitive moat {#moat}
  A moat is the reason profits are not competed away. The evidence is high returns on capital held for many years.
  - Brand {#N56dla6W0Fjg}
  - Network effects {#HMDLunpbirv0}
  - Switching costs {#q2uskz2aDsnL}
  - Cost or scale advantage {#DU7BZZHmTkNT}
  - Patents and licences {#bZZhtBIlCbSd}
- Management {#mgmt}
  - Track record {#8i9uouMI0Avl}
    - Past performance {#AjrtzS5kNEpN}
      - Revenue growth {#Jr8Txq7gUcGl}
      - Profitability {#HgrZ6RwdqQKV}
      - Market share {#rDjgwWwWaT4v}
    - Promise delivery {#3tnmDK3SCv3L}
      - Targets met {#Gs7DXCjQSQxG}
      - Guidance accuracy {#u662Tks0UKnU}
    - Crisis response {#uxFUwP5K2eTN}
      - Downturn resilience {#Xr9TLj9BZcBL}
      - Recovery speed {#EESLOWg6V0xO}
  - Capital allocation {#ATRoqAAKbpWl}
    What they do with spare cash: reinvest, acquire, repay debt, pay dividends or buy back shares.
  - Insider ownership {#cYNznXI73vNp}
  - Pay tied to returns {#a3U17hZwYmNM}

## 2 Read the filings {#filings sticker=📄 side=right}

Primary sources first. Read the company before you read opinions about it.

- Annual report (10-K) {#annual}
  - Business description {#XTvDrLLcVj99}
  - Risk factors {#OPA2P4g5Jqpq}
  - Management discussion (MD&#38;A) {#8LnuIpQKMqUw}
  - Notes to the accounts {#5zfbn57esI7r}
    Where the accounting choices live: revenue recognition, leases, pensions, segment detail.
- Quarterly report (10-Q) {#TUDm8WedYkuJ}
- Earnings call and slides {#X1lItEQbBzkG}
  Listen for what analysts keep asking about, and for changes in tone or guidance.
- Proxy statement {#FFpi8ADgkMuF}
  - Executive pay {#dIfYQAzKHpk3}
  - Share ownership {#g05TxGr7zwPZ}
- Read five years back {#EC6dPb7JExye}
  Compare what management promised with what happened.

## 3 Income statement {#income sticker=💵 side=right}

What the company earned over a period.

- Revenue {#revenue}
  - Growth rate over 5 years {#VrrPNndiWxTu}
  - Organic vs acquired growth {#jtWnb32ivi1m}
  - Price vs volume {#ci4MJk5wMnF0}
- Gross margin {#DfOYe71iyBQe}
  Gross profit ÷ revenue. Shows pricing power and the cost of making the product.
- Operating margin {#opmargin}
  Operating profit ÷ revenue. A margin that rises as sales rise shows operating leverage.
- Net income {#netincome}
  - One-off items {#5FXlCDIietTE}
  - Adjusted vs reported {#ZTsIJhF9TS6k}
    Be wary when adjusted profit is always higher than reported profit.
  - Tax rate {#a2xmRpCLm8F3}
- Earnings per share {#eps}
  - Share count trend {#cXYK1jBgJXBp}
  - Dilution from stock pay {#j85q5MGih50i}
  - Buybacks {#ThGLLrtw6Db4}

## 4 Balance sheet {#balance sticker=⚖️ side=right}

What the company owns and owes on one day.

- Liquidity {#EGik9uGx8FXs}
  - Cash and equivalents {#ES4G82uQavDf}
  - Current ratio {#XDkCxb2jedfk}
    Current assets ÷ current liabilities. Above 1 means short-term bills are covered.
  - Quick ratio {#rQboZ7Fv5qW0}
    (Cash + receivables) ÷ current liabilities.
- Debt {#debt}
  - Net debt to EBITDA {#1UNApohyTOVe}
    (Debt − cash) ÷ EBITDA. Above 3 deserves a hard look in most industries.
  - Interest coverage {#Lr1wm4wFKZmw}
    Operating profit ÷ interest expense. Below 3 leaves little room for a bad year.
  - Maturity schedule {#9YOvOKF7YIwD}
  - Fixed vs floating rate {#Id89K0gQbVpG}
- Working capital {#wc}
  - Receivable days {#XmHBwbB3XD49}
  - Inventory days {#e5Nvj7fNgWOY}
  - Payable days {#QWqorGNOTDyY}
  - Cash conversion cycle {#fsWv1AAplZKg}
    Inventory days + receivable days − payable days. Shorter is better.
- Asset quality {#xeq8JwCISNjA}
  - Goodwill and intangibles {#wdLgrwnfTYW0}
  - Write-down history {#BOMknoKaoFYT}
- Equity {#Di2tAcy68oMT}
  - Book value per share {#RSlqvjq7TIJj}
  - Retained earnings {#j44oO5tAXxcK}

## 5 Cash flow {#cash sticker=💧 side=left}

Cash is harder to flatter than profit. Trust it more.

- Operating cash flow {#ocf}
  - Cash conversion {#4dCwnKHreukU}
    Operating cash flow ÷ net income. Near or above 1 over several years is healthy.
  - Working capital swings {#YuNxDrkR15RO}
- Capital spending {#capex}
  - Maintenance capex {#CP7RbYYSMhDc}
  - Growth capex {#bV3eUUZUn2vP}
- Free cash flow {#fcf}
  Operating cash flow − capital spending. The cash owners could take out each year.
  - FCF margin {#15EsNniFB4Wz}
  - FCF per share {#sIayZxuPE8HE}
  - Stock pay as a cost {#7BmfCr4Blmze}
    Stock-based pay is added back in the cash flow statement, but it is a real cost to owners.
- Uses of cash {#uses}
  - Dividends {#9bECmj2cI1JC}
  - Buybacks {#BhF7ogduCALz}
  - Acquisitions {#F65bLXNSOyT0}
  - Debt repayment {#2FC1XjR63OtD}

## 6 Quality and returns {#quality sticker=⭐ side=left}

Good businesses earn high returns on the money put into them, year after year.

- Return on equity {#roe}
  Net income ÷ shareholders’ equity. Split it three ways (DuPont) to see what drives it.
  - Net margin {#SJM1oCzab4Nl}
  - Asset turnover {#aohZPiLD804E}
  - Financial leverage {#9bmdbCSkArer}
- Return on invested capital {#roic}
  After-tax operating profit ÷ (debt + equity − cash). Compare it with the cost of capital.
  - ROIC above cost of capital {#XDpX9u2oNLQz}
  - Trend over ten years {#2lzM3YMuqR3f}
- Growth quality {#Ot7BUfx6OSyT}
  - Reinvestment rate {#fiWxKfwnabCL}
  - Return on new investment {#LfVdGEbYVvLq}
- Earnings quality {#equality}
  - Accruals {#thYDsLPsodHU}
    Profit far above cash flow for years is a warning.
  - Receivables vs sales growth {#i1rn2Em2QBsd}
  - Capitalised costs {#Gfqo7zZTLAvm}
- Compare with peers {#qhUBVB32S0qo}

## 7 Valuation {#valuation sticker=🏷️ side=left}

Price is what you pay; value is what the business is worth. Estimate value several ways.

- Multiples {#multiples}
  Quick and relative. Compare with peers and with the company’s own history.
  - Price to earnings (P/E) {#ceCJB3tn3g0D}
  - PEG ratio {#ww4x7aHGWrig}
    P/E ÷ expected growth rate.
  - EV to EBITDA {#ZnM0qrEd6yO1}
    Enterprise value = market value + debt − cash.
  - Price to free cash flow {#4KACp68JLyZM}
  - Price to book {#9kFXJfDkwp22}
  - EV to sales {#6WXptAC1vCfK}
- Discounted cash flow {#dcf}
  Value = the sum of future free cash flows, each discounted back to today.
  - Forecast free cash flow {#MG5ywVrl0nnf}
  - Discount rate (WACC) {#naL3PYVIQqCZ}
  - Terminal value {#bX6yVkq2TA3A}
  - Sensitivity table {#8W4dWQ6Cu7VG}
- Reverse DCF {#tbGxW2A1VslT}
  Ask what growth the current price already assumes, then judge whether that is likely.
- Dividend discount model {#d4WkdKhC1gxj}
- Sum of the parts {#pTbRhAxFfNpv}
- Margin of safety {#mos}
  Buy only when the price is well below your estimate, so that mistakes are affordable.

## 8 Risks and red flags {#risks sticker=🚩 side=left}

Look for reasons not to buy before you look for reasons to buy.

- Business risks {#oTNBfejPMdhC}
  - Disruption {#jOD3fegHrpJF}
  - Regulation and lawsuits {#b8JGBCQHy8Wn}
  - Cyclical demand {#s8TNJYYGAZGy}
  - Key supplier or customer {#PwLqAq0imvOR}
- Financial risks {#finrisk}
  - High leverage {#XTnv54DcVKsv}
  - Refinancing wall {#kJfWOPPQUQui}
  - Currency and rates {#4Qoc2jj3GPXd}
- Accounting red flags {#redflags}
  - Profit up, cash down {#DDxFDQuq3FeA}
  - Frequent one-off charges {#nMwYtJEK1z3h}
  - Auditor or CFO changes {#vN4fmOBUrzNC}
  - Aggressive revenue recognition {#zgzt7HPf3xOq}
- Governance {#pWlgUwxbYfAw}
  - Related-party deals {#KFgVkMynGwD7}
  - Dual-class shares {#o5LjetsWZv3p}
  - Heavy dilution {#Pg3U8i39znuT}

## 9 Decide and monitor {#decide sticker=✅ side=left}

Write the decision down so you can check it later against what happened.

- Investment thesis {#thesis}
  Three reasons to own it, written before you buy.
  - What must go right {#vTdd8TDi4GoD}
  - What the market misses {#p3uOCFcZ8yW8}
- Scenarios {#scenarios}
  - Bear case {#AaSYJfnd740K}
  - Base case {#c6Ir3iExTotT}
  - Bull case {#V8Rjg2C0E7D9}
  - Probability-weighted value {#gzpSjw471Yd5}
- Position size {#0OjDZzV0O10b}
  Size by how wrong you could be, not by how sure you feel.
- Catalysts {#LKSFNzmrE8KT}
- Sell rules {#sell}
  - Thesis broken {#t93QRKb2B3tq}
  - Price above value {#gHIEyFLoFAVO}
  - Better idea found {#w2fwPZptioZr}
- Track each quarter {#w5PQd9M3EhQ6}
  - Key numbers vs thesis {#xmqm3AByaFUs}
  - Decision journal {#giTS0ZmVC9HU}
