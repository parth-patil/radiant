## (a) History and precursors of mind mapping

### Takeaway
Diagrammatic hierarchies are ancient (Porphyry, 3rd century; Llull, 1235-1315). The term "mind map" and the colourful radial style are tied to Buzan's 1974 BBC series *Use Your Head*. Concept maps (Novak, Cornell, 1970s) are a distinct, later-or-parallel tradition: freer networks with labelled links. Several requested precursors (Leonardo notebooks, Darwin's tree sketch, Evelyn Wood, Collins/Quillian, Rico's clustering) could not be sourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Porphyry of Tyros (3rd century) graphically visualised Aristotle's categories, among the earliest such diagrams; Ramon Llull (1235-1315) used similar techniques. — [Wikipedia: Mind map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_map)
- 1974: Buzan hosted the BBC series *Use Your Head* and promoted a radial, tree-like diagram of colourful keywords; Wikipedia says this is where the term "mind map" was introduced. — [Wikipedia: Mind map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_map)
- The Times obituary is cited by Wikipedia as saying Buzan's technique was inspired by Leonardo da Vinci, Albert Einstein and Novak's concept mapping (this is a claim about Buzan's inspiration; the primary Times text was not read). Serig (2011) is quoted as disputing his invention claim: "the idea that he invented them is quite preposterous." — [Wikipedia: Mind map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_map)
- Buzan's own origin story, as told in the Irish Times (2015): he noticed students who doodled on notes got good exam results, was frustrated by note-taking time while studying at UBC in the 1960s, and found that doodling had been used by geniuses such as Leonardo and Einstein. — [Irish Times, 1 June 2015](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/how-tony-buzan-used-mind-maps-to-doodle-his-way-to-millions-1.2230977)
- Concept maps: developed in the 1970s by Joseph D. Novak and his team at Cornell to represent students' emerging science knowledge, tied to constructivism and to Ausubel's 1968 *Educational Psychology: A Cognitive View*. Novak and Gowin published *Learning How to Learn* in 1984. The first International Conference on Concept Mapping was in Pamplona in 2004. — [Wikipedia: Concept map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_map). The Irish Times dates Novak's concept mapping at Cornell to 1972 — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/how-tony-buzan-used-mind-maps-to-doodle-his-way-to-millions-1.2230977)
- Mind map vs concept map: mind maps are a tree with one central concept; concept maps are free-form, can have several hubs and usually label links with text. Lanzing (1998): "a mind map has only one main concept, while a concept map may have several." — [Wikipedia: Mind map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_map), [Wikipedia: Concept map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_map)
- Context: Edward de Bono coined "lateral thinking" in the 1960s; Pelmanism was a large correspondence mind-training business in early 1900s Britain. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/how-tony-buzan-used-mind-maps-to-doodle-his-way-to-millions-1.2230977)

### Inferences
- For the app: the "radial tree, single centre" constraint is what separates a Buzan mind map from a concept map; cross-links (Buzan's arrows/codes) are the allowed exception.

### Gaps
- No source found for Leonardo's notebooks as precursor beyond Buzan's own claim, Darwin's 1837 tree sketch, Evelyn Wood, Collins and Quillian (1960s semantic networks), Gabriele Rico's clustering (Writing the Natural Way, 1983 from memory; unverified), or spider diagrams' origin. Wikipedia pages fetched did not cover them.

## (b) Buzan's biography and popularisation

### Takeaway
Anthony Peter "Tony" Buzan, 2 June 1942 to 13 April 2019, British author and educational consultant; built a large commercial brand (books, instructors, software, memory championships) around mind maps.

### Cited Findings
- Born 2 June 1942 in Palmers Green, Middlesex; died 13 April 2019, aged 76, at John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford (heart attack). Brother is academic Barry Buzan. — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan)
- Education: Kitsilano Secondary School, Vancouver; University of British Columbia (psychology, English, mathematics, science); Simon Fraser University 1965-66 (charter student, first president of its student society); Mensa member and editor of the *International Journal of Mensa*. — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan)
- The BBC series *Use Your Head* (1970s; 1974 per Wikipedia Mind map page) led to five books: *Use Your Memory*, *Master Your Memory*, *Use Your Head*, *The Speed Reading Book*, *The Mind Map Book*; sold over 3 million by 2003; 80+ books overall. Also *Speed Reading* (1971), *Use Your Perfect Memory* (1991), *Mind Map Mastery* (2018). — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan)
- Wikipedia lists the Buzan Organisation as holding trademarks on "Mind Maps" for self-improvement courses in the UK, USA and Germany. — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan)
- Co-founded/involved in World Memory Championships and World Championships of the Brain; co-founded the Mind Sports Olympiad and Mind Body Spirit Festival. — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan). (The 1991 date and Raymond Keene co-founding were in the brief but not confirmed by a source I read.)
- Business per Irish Times (2015): books, DVDs, seminars and schools in Mumbai, Dubai, Singapore, Tokyo and Henley-on-Thames; 120+ books in 30 languages "worth well over EUR 150 million"; 700+ ThinkBuzan licensed instructors accredited since 2010; mind map described as his trademark. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/how-tony-buzan-used-mind-maps-to-doodle-his-way-to-millions-1.2230977). Note the Irish Times and Wikipedia disagree on book counts (120+ vs 80+).
- Phil Chambers: described as World Mind Mapping Champion, Buzan-trained instructor since 1995; his *101 Top Tips for Better Mind Maps* targets hand-drawn maps. — [Mind Mapping Software Blog review](https://mindmappingsoftwareblog.com/101-top-tips-for-better-mind-maps/)

### Gaps
- Guardian obituary could not be fetched (blocked); Times/BBC obits not read. *The Mind Map Book* (1993, with Barry Buzan) date, *How to Mind Map* (2002) date and Raymond Keene/1991 championship details unverified here. Barry Buzan's co-authorship role not sourced.

## (c) Buzan's rules/laws and reasons

### Takeaway
Buzan's rules cluster as Emphasis, Association, Clarity (technique) and layout rules (hierarchy, numerical order, personal style). Only a few stated reasons were found in accessible sources. All lists below are SECONDARY paraphrases of Buzan, not verified primary text.

### Cited Findings
Version 1: ten guidelines attributed to Buzan on Creativity at Work (Linda Naiman's page; quoted as listed there):
1. "Start in the center with an image of the topic, using at least 3 colors."
2. "Use images, symbols, codes, and dimensions throughout your mind map."
3. "Select key words and print using upper or lower case letters."
4. "Each word/image is best alone and sitting on its own line."
5. "The lines should be connected, starting from the central image." / "The lines become thinner as they radiate out from the center."
6. "Make the lines the same length as the word/image they support."
7. "Use multiple colors throughout the mind map, for visual stimulation and also for encoding or grouping."
8. "Develop your own personal style of mind mapping."
9. "Use emphasis and show associations in your mind map."
10. "Keep the mind map clear by using radial hierarchy or outlines to embrace your branches."
— [Creativity at Work](https://www.creativityatwork.com/?p=3186). Only 7 and 10 give a reason (visual stimulation/encoding/grouping; clarity). Note rule 3 here says upper OR lower case, which conflicts with the commonly cited "printed capitals" advice.

Version 2: slide-deck summary crediting *The Mind Map Book* (edition/pages not given; paraphrase) — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mind-map-laws-and-recommendations/14624301):
- Emphasis: always a central image; images throughout; three or more colours per central image; dimension in images; synaesthesia; vary size of printing, lines and images; organised and appropriate spacing.
- Association: arrows to connect within and across branches; colours to enhance memory and creativity; codes to make instant connections between different parts of the map.
- Be clear: one keyword per line (reason given: single words help you see your internal and external environment more clearly and realistically); print all words; print keywords on lines; line length equals word length; connect lines to other lines; central lines thicker; boundaries embrace outline of branches; make images clear; paper horizontal (landscape); keep printing upright.
- Personal style: reflect your own network of thought; hierarchy; numerical order.
- Break mental blocks: blank lines, questions, images, review, quick mind-map checks.
- Prepare: positive attitude, "commit to the absurd", make the map as beautiful as possible; comfortable workspace (moderate temperature, natural light, fresh air, music or silence).
- Mistakes to avoid: structure becoming random, monotonous, disassociated, or flattened to one level, which "aren't really Mind Maps"; do not assume phrases are more meaningful than single words; a messy map is not necessarily bad (reflects your state of mind); a map is a first draft that needs revision.

Version 3: seven steps (mnemonic "PICCCKI" on one user-made XMind template): Page (landscape, blank), central Image, Colours, Connect branches, Curved branches, Keywords, Images. Curved lines preferred because "straight lines bore the brain" (a paraphrase by a search summary, not a verified Buzan quote); one keyword per branch, not sentences. Nottingham University study-skills page is the most credible of the sources returned. — [Nottingham mindmaps steps](https://www.nottingham.ac.uk/helmopen/rlos/study-skills/critical-reflection/other_methods/mindmaps_steps.html), [XMind template](https://templates.xmind.com/templates/mind-map-7-steps-hujr) (Nottingham page itself not opened; only seen in search results)

Version 4: PMI exhibit "Rules of Mind Mapping (Buzan, 2006)": central image, one main topic per branch, sub-topics on their own sub-branches, three or more colours. — [PMI](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/mind-mapping-advantage-think-creatively-6554)

Other: ProjectWizards frames technique principles as emphasis, association, clarity, plus "rules of design". — [ProjectWizards](https://www.projectwizards.net/en/blog/2018/08/mindmapping). A reviewer calls the rules "more like a supporting set of principles" than strict laws. — [Adam Tornhill review](https://adamtornhill.com/reviews/mindmapbook.htm)

### Inferences
- Style rules for an AI generator that all versions agree on: single central image with 3+ colours; one keyword per branch; branch length matches word; thick-to-thin tapering; colour-coded branches; images on branches; landscape orientation; hierarchy with central branches as main themes.
- Stated reasons are thin; most reasoning must be taken from the generic claims in section (d).

### Gaps
- Exact wording of the "Laws of Mind Mapping" in *The Mind Map Book* (technique vs layout), the 7 steps in *How to Mind Map*, and any distinct rules in *Mind Map Mastery* (2018) were not obtained. Reasons for curved branches, capitals, line length not directly sourced. The "Concise, Connect, Color, Clear, Cartoons, Clockwise, Creative" XMind template is a third-party mnemonic, not Buzan.

## (d) Radiant thinking, memorability and eye-catching design; "non mind maps"

### Takeaway
Buzan framed radiant thinking as the brain's natural association-driven mode, which mind maps are meant to mirror; memorability claims rest on imagery, colour, humour, exaggeration and association. Most neuroscientific framing is disputed.

### Cited Findings
- Radiant thinking "is intended to reflect the structure and processes of our brains"; maps always radiate from a central image, giving a hierarchical, non-linear view. — [Adam Tornhill review of The Mind Map Book](https://adamtornhill.com/reviews/mindmapbook.htm)
- Buzan treats imagery as the brain's main language and memory as working through images, locations and connections. — [Creativity at Work](https://www.creativityatwork.com/?p=3186)
- SMASHIN' SCOPE (memory principles, credited to Buzan and Vanda North; The Memory Book): Synaesthesia/Sensuality, Movement, Association, Sexuality, Humour, Imagination, Numbers, Symbolism, Colour, Order/Sequence, Positive images, Exaggeration. — [Art of Memory wiki via search summary](https://artofmemory.com/wiki/SMASHIN_SCOPE/) (page itself returned 403; list taken from search snippet), [Learning Subwiki](https://learning.subwiki.org/wiki/Smashin_scope)
- Review schedule: Buzan quoted (via O'Reilly book) recommending review shortly after learning, then one day, one week, one month, and three to six months later. A GoConqr summary of *The Memory Book* adds a 10-minute review. — [GoConqr](https://goconqr.com/mind_maps/7954145), [O'Reilly](https://oreilly.com/library/view/pomodoro-technique-illustrated/9781680500103/f_0111.html). The "10 minutes" step is weakly sourced.
- "Bad" maps per the Mind Map Book summary: random, monotonous, disassociated, one-level maps "aren't really Mind Maps"; phrases are not more meaningful than single words. — [SlideShare summary](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mind-map-laws-and-recommendations/14624301). No direct Buzan quotes on boxes, monochrome, straight lines or computer uniformity were found.

### Inferences
- Memorability style rules to encode: distinctive central image, colour variety, dimension, humour/absurdity ("commit to the absurd"), varied line/letter size, spaced layout; a review/revisit feature at ~1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 3-6 months.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes on proto-mind-maps, boxes or computer-generated uniformity. Method of loci/Major system links, humour/exaggeration reasoning, and BOST not sourced.

## (e) Uses and software legacy

### Takeaway
iMindMap (Buzan with Chris Griffiths, 2006) aimed to reproduce hand-drawn organic maps; it evolved into the cloud tool Ayoa under OpenGenius.

### Cited Findings
- iMindMap launched December 2006 with Chris Griffiths (Wikipedia); a beta in November 2006 and version 1 in January 2007 is also reported; Ayoa pages differ (2006 vs 2007). — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan), [Mind Mapping Software Blog](https://mindmappingsoftwareblog.com/imindmap-a-closer-look/)
- iMindMap was built to "preserve visuality, speed and flexibility" of hand-drawn maps; Ayoa is OpenGenius's cloud successor with Griffiths as CEO and AI features; the article says maps are "best done by both hand and digitally". — [Ayoa blog](https://www.ayoa.com/ourblog/from-paper-to-imindmap-to-ai-the-continued-evolution-of-mind-mapping/). Vendor source, not neutral. Ayoa launch date (2019) unconfirmed.
- Irish Times: iMindMap prices $80 (home/student) to $245 (Ultimate), focused on quick-draw branch editing. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/how-tony-buzan-used-mind-maps-to-doodle-his-way-to-millions-1.2230977)
- A reviewer says the final chapter of *The Mind Map Book* "reads like a sales brochure" for Buzan's software and lacks trade-off discussion of digitisation. — [Adam Tornhill](https://adamtornhill.com/reviews/mindmapbook.htm)
- Other practitioners: Jamie Nast (*Idea Mapping*, trainer) — [Mind Mapping Software Blog](https://mindmappingsoftwareblog.com/blog/page/6/) (listed in search only).

### Gaps
- Buzan's own statements on computer vs hand-drawn not found. Uses (BOST, master/mini/group maps), Griffiths, Colliar, O'Brien, Gelb, Wycoff, Margulies, Vanda North's laws: no usable sources found.

## (f) Criticism

### Takeaway
Critics call the left/right brain and "10% of the brain" framing fallacious, note Buzan rarely cites evidence, dispute his invention claim, and question commercialisation; empirical results for mind maps are modest and mixed.

### Cited Findings
- Cynics call brain lateralisation (relied on by Buzan) a scientific fallacy; critics say the "10% of brain" idea is wrong; Joshua Foer says Buzan has a "habit of lapsing into pseudoscience and hyperbole" and that a claim of thinking-tool use dropping from 98% in young children to under 15% in industry is where "credibility gives way to a sales pitch". — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/how-tony-buzan-used-mind-maps-to-doodle-his-way-to-millions-1.2230977) (note: the article says Buzan's ideas "rely on" the 10% claim; this is the journalist's/critics' framing, not verified from Buzan's text)
- Brain regions are not as specific as left/right claims suggest. — [EBSCO Research Starters via search summary](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/mind-map-diagram)
- Reviewer: Buzan "rarely references any scientific support for his claims". — [Adam Tornhill](https://adamtornhill.com/reviews/mindmapbook.htm)
- Research (all via Wikipedia summary): Farrand et al. (2002) spider diagrams raised recall of a 600-word text by 10% vs 6% for preferred methods, gain only after a week, lower motivation; Nesbit and Adesope (2006) meta-analysis found concept mapping beat reading/lectures, with significant heterogeneity; Beel and Langer (2011): average user made 2.7 maps of 31 nodes. — [Wikipedia: Mind map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_map)
- Trademark: "Mind Maps" trademark in UK/USA/Germany for courses. — [Wikipedia: Tony Buzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Buzan)

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed rebuttal specifically of "radiant thinking" found; no source on disagreement over one-word-per-branch (Novak/others) or detailed trademark criticism.
