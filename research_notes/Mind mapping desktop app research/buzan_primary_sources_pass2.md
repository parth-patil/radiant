## 1. The primary text of Buzan's rules, and his reasons

### Takeaway
The only verified rule list I could read is the 2018 ten-Law list in *Mind Map Mastery* (restated from the earlier "Laws of Technique/Layout"), plus an earlier seven-advantage and four-guideline set in *Use Your Head* (1984, pp. 91-93, 109-110). Both give reasons. The Mind Map Book's two-part Laws (emphasis, association, clarity, personal style; hierarchy, numerical order) remain secondary-only (pass-one section (c)).

### Cited Findings: MMM18 "The Laws of Mind Mapping" (Chapter 2, book p. 60), verbatim

Laws 1-10, copied from [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf):
1. "Always use a blank sheet of paper, placed in landscape position. Make sure the sheet is large enough to allow you to create subbranches and sub-sub-branches."
2. "Draw a picture in the centre of the paper, representing your subject, using at least three colours."
3. "Use images, symbols, codes and dimension throughout your Mind Map."
4. "Select keywords and write these using capital letters."
5. "Place each word or image on its own branch, so that it stands by itself."
6. "Radiate flowing branches out from the central image. Make the branches thicker toward the centre of the Mind Map, and thinner as they radiate outward into subbranches."
7. "Keep branches the same length as the words or images on them."
8. "Use colours throughout the Mind Map, developing your own colour code in the branches."
9. "Use emphasis, arrows and connecting lines to depict associations between different related topics in your Mind Map."
10. "Aim for clarity in your Mind Map by positioning your branches in carefully thought-through space. Remember that the space between things is often as important as the things themselves. Imagine, for example, the space between trees in a forest: your brain negotiates these gaps to understand where you are and where you are going, rather than the trees."

Framing in the same chapter (MMM18 ch. 2): the Laws "are designed to support your thinking with a structure, allowing it to flourish rather than to get tangled up and flounder." "If you deviate from them, you won't create a true Mind Map." Following them "frees you up to develop your own unique 'fingerprint', or rather 'eyeprint', style". He suggests keeping a copy of the Laws "perhaps in the form of a Mind Map" and says that by Mind Map number 100 you will have "attained a highly skilled level". Ch. 4 repeats that the Laws "are a manifestation of the more general laws of good thinking".

### Cited Findings: Buzan's stated reasons, in MMM18 ch. 2 (verbatim unless marked)

- **Landscape paper:** "the information on a horizontal Mind Map is easier to absorb at a glance, as it allows you to scan the entire page in a non-linear fashion, radiating from the centre, rather than reading the page left to right, left to right, left to right – like watching tennis, getting a stiff neck". Plain unlined paper "doesn't feature lines or any decoration that will detract from your creative process." Also "Keep your Mind Map as upright as you can, with its branches as close to the horizontal as possible, which will make the Mind Map much easier to read and recall." (printing/branches upright, from the Mind Map Book rule "printing as upright as possible")
- **Central image:** "A dynamic central image will automatically focus the eye and the brain, triggering numerous associations." He asks for dimension, self-expression and at least three colours; if a word is central, "convert the word itself into an image by using dimension and colours". Ch. 4 checklist: "If the central image includes a word, is that word in 3D or made to look interesting typographically?"
- **Basic Ordering Ideas (BOIs):** main branches are "known as Basic Ordering Ideas (BOIs) and constitute the core framework"; prompts include "the most important seven categories", chapter headings, and "What? Where? Who? How? Which? When?". Suggested BOI lenses: structure, function, properties, processes, evaluation, definitions, classification, history, personalities.
- **Thick-to-thin branches:** "make the branches closest to the central image thicker, to emphasize their importance to your brain". "The most general (inclusive) concepts tend to sit closer to the central image". If an outer idea proves more important, "simply thicken up the outer branches where necessary".
- **Curved, organic branches:** "Make all the branches reach out in an organic, wave-like, flowing manner to enhance their visual appeal. In addition ... if you make your branches curve and radiate organically, each one will have a unique shape which can then be further used to activate your recall of the information held within that branch." Ch. 3 lists "only straight lines" as a failure: "think of its rigidity and its lack of appeal." The famous slogan "straight lines are boring to your brain" was NOT found in either book (see Gaps); the verified reasons are visual appeal, unique shape for recall, and rigidity.
- **Images:** "image making engages a wide range of cortical skills, from imagination, logic and spatial awareness to the use of colour, form, line dimension and visual patterning" (vs linear notes' "linear patterning, symbols and analysis"). Images need not be good: "quick descriptive sketches, drawings, symbols and doodles all work well!" and "think of your illustrations as a form of shorthand". He cites Haber (1970): shown 10,000 photographs the average person "can remember more than 98 percent". "Make your images as clear as possible ... A clear image will lead to a clear response." The phrase "a picture is worth a thousand words" does not appear in the extracted text.
- **One keyword per branch ("PLAY WITH WORDS"):** "For clarity, impact and freedom, remember to use only one keyword on each branch ... A single keyword is a lot easier to remember than a phrase and will lodge in the memory. Like a pebble dropped in a pool, it will ripple out and trigger lots of different associations ... by focusing on one word per branch, you are forced to deliberate about precisely which word best represents your idea ... actively engaging your powers of discrimination and analysis". UYH84 p. 92 gives the earlier reason: "Words should be in 'units', i.e. one word per line. This leaves each word more free hooks and gives note-taking more freedom and flexibility".
- **Branch length equals word length:** "A keyword should always sit on a branch of the same length. This will allow you to place a number of words in close proximity to each other ..., thereby encouraging you to find even more associations as the words 'bounce' off each other. It will also make your Mind Map appear less cluttered and allow you to include more information in it." Ch. 3: "Imagine your Mind Map branches and keywords are of unequal length: see how its structure collapses."
- **Capitals:** "Write the words in capitals to give them definition and to make it easier for your mind to visualize them ... you can use upper and lower case letters in the subbranches to show the relative importance of words". UYH84 p. 92: capitals because "a printed map gives a more photographic, more immediate, and more comprehensive feed-back. The little extra time that it takes to print is amply made up for in the time saved when reading back." (This resolves pass-one's upper-or-lower-case conflict: Buzan says capitals, relaxing to lowercase only in outer sub-branches.)
- **Words on lines, lines connected:** UYH84 p. 92: "The printed words should be on lines, and each line should be connected to other lines. This is to guarantee that the mind map has basic structure."
- **Size as hierarchy:** "The larger an item, the greater its visual impact and the more likely you will be to recall it." "Write any words associated with [the most important elements] in THREE DIMENSIONS."
- **Synaesthesia and movement:** he argues that "well-organized synaesthesia can actually increase the powers of the brain. A Mind Map can be considered an organized synaesthetic thinking tool". Use "all five senses in your words and images" and suggest "movement ... through the organic form of the branches".
- **Colour codes:** "create your own colour codes by matching specific colours to your BOIs ... will help you access the information in your Mind Map more quickly, improve your memory of it, and increase the number and range of your creative ideas." Example: Wu Xing, Wood green, Fire orange, Earth brown, Metal silver, Water blue. Colour is also tied to the von Restorff effect (ch. 1, p. 40, not in the extract). Black-and-white first drafts are allowed but "colour is a key element in promoting creativity ... if you initially create your Mind Map in black and white, you will be radically limiting your ability to generate new ideas."
- **Codes:** simple (crosses, ticks, underlines, circles, triangles, squares) or "elaborate symbols – even shown in 3D", like footnote symbols and the Major System code; they "reinforce the categorization and hierarchy". UYH84 p. 109 had the same devices: arrows, codes (asterisks, exclamation marks, crosses, question marks), geometrical shapes (a square for the main centre, oblongs next, triangles after), artistic three dimension ("ideas ... will thus 'stand off the page'"), creativity/images, colour.
- **Connections and arrows:** arrows and connecting branches (curves, circles, loops, chains) "link up separate areas and branches" and "automatically direct your eye ... give spatial direction to your thoughts, promoting divergent and highly creative thinking". They "can vary in size, form and dimension" and "can also be multi-headed".
- **Boundaries and chunking:** from his 1950s-60s "Brain Pattern Notes" with "colourful boundaries ... a wavy line or a cloud-like bubble"; later related to Miller (1956) chunking. "In Mind Mapping, the outline of a boundary creates a unique shape of its own, making it memorable in its own right". Caution: "If a branch is outlined with a boundary too soon, the Mind Mapper will potentially place themselves in a restrictive prison."
- **Space:** "The right amount of space around each item will give your Mind Map clarity and structure, and make it more visually appealing – which in turn means that you are more likely to absorb the information in it."
- **Repetition rule (not in the Laws):** a word repeating across branches is "not a problem. This is a breakthrough"; underline it, box at 3 repeats, 3-D boxes at 4+, and "use the word ... as the new central image in your next Mind Map" (ch. 4).
- **Mini and master maps:** "Mini Mind Maps orbit the main Mind Map like moons around a planet. They explore aspects of the main Mind Map in closer detail without cluttering it up." If no room, give them "a page of their own" (ch. 4). UYH84 p. 148 also advises "enormous 'Central' Mind Maps which summarise and overview the main branches and structures of your subject areas".
- **Time-box:** "Mind Mapping is most effective when undertaken in bursts of about 20 minutes."

Source for all of the above: [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf), ch. 2-4.

### Cited Findings: UYH84 (1984) mind-map guidance, verbatim
- Seven advantages of a mind map over linear notes (UYH84 pp. 91-92, ch. 4 "Noting" part B): "1 The centre or main idea is more clearly defined. 2 The relative importance of each idea is clearly indicated. More important ideas will be nearer the centre and less important ideas will be near the edge. 3 The links between the key concepts will be immediately recognisable because of their proximity and connection. 4 ... recall and review will be both more effective and more rapid. 5 The nature of the structure allows for the easy addition of new information without messy scratching out or squeezing in ... 6 Each map made will look and be different from each other map. This will aid recall. 7 ... the open-ended nature of the map will enable the brain to make new connections far more readily." Source: [UYH84 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934504509-6qustd.pdf).
- The method: "Rather than starting from the top and working down in sentences or lists, one should start from the centre or main idea and branch out as dictated by the individual ideas and general form of the central theme." (p. 91)
- Keep the mind free: "Any 'thinking' about where things should go ... will simply slow down the process ... Do not worry about order or organisation as this will in many cases take care of itself." (pp. 92-93). "With the map approach each idea is left as a totally open possibility, so that the map grows organically and increasingly, rather than being stifled." (p. 95)
- Why key words (pp. 82-83): in conventional "good" notes "90 per cent of the words are not necessary for recall purposes". Six costs of sentence notes include "The connections between key words are interrupted by words that separate them. We know that memory works by association" and key words "separated in time" and "separated in space". Single words matter because "the single key word or phrase will bring back whole ranges of experience and sensation. Think for example of the range of images that enter your mind when you read the word 'child'." Note he says "word or phrase" here in 1984.
- "Messy" notes (p. 114): "The word 'messy' used in this way refers to the look and not to the content ... The notes which look 'neat' are, in informational terms, messy." Mapped notes "seldom take more than ten minutes to finalise an hour's notes on a fresh sheet of paper" and this redraw "will fit in perfectly as the first review".
- Uses (pp. 112-115): speech/essay planning ("students were able to complete essays in one third of the previous time while receiving higher marks" at Oxford), two-page note-taking (left page mapped, right page linear/graphic), and meetings run on a board-based mind map.
- Rationale from brain science, in the 1984 framing: a note-taking technique "designed to satisfy the needs of the whole brain would have to include not only words, numbers, order, sequence, and lines, but also colour, images, dimension, symbols, and visual rhythms etc: in other words Mind Maps" (p. 110, citing Sperry and Ornstein). Note the term "Radiant Thinking" does not occur in UYH84.

### Cited Findings: how the 2018 text frames the origin
- Origin story: he underlined keywords in his notes and "realized they made up less than 10 percent of what I had written down. Yet these keywords unlocked core concepts" (Introduction). He drew on the method of loci and Major System. Linear notes in "blue or black ink" are "by default boring, which means your brain will tune out, dial down and eventually go to sleep" (the verified form of the "boring" claim; it concerns monochrome notes, not curves). Neuron model: each neuron "has tentacles radiating out from the cell's centre like the branches of a tree" (Preface). He dates the Mind Map to "since its inception in the mid-1960s" (Preface) while saying "I introduced Mind Maps to the world in the 1960s" (Introduction). Precursors by his own account: "Brain Pattern Notes in the 1950s and 1960s", initially called "Buzan Diagrams" (Jezz Moore anecdote), with Lorraine Gill's challenge to "reappraise the role that pictures and colours played".
- Mind map vs linear note-taking table (Introduction): Linear/Multi-faceted; Monochrome/Colourful; Word-based/Words combined with pictures; Listed logic/Associated logic; Sequential/Multidimensional; Restrictive/Imaginative; Disorganized/Analytical.
- Mind maps are "analytical in the sense that you can use them to solve any problem", "on the one hand microcosmic and on the other macrocosmic".
- Source: [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf), Preface, Introduction.

### Cited Findings: secondary-only (Mind Map Book Laws)
- The Mind Map Book Laws of Technique and Layout, the "three As", and the "break mental blocks / reinforce / prepare" recommendations: pass-one section (c) gives the SlideShare paraphrase ([SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/mind-map-laws-and-recommendations/14624301)). Follow the Laws ... until you become completely familiar with them"), **Application** ("apply them by creating as many Mind Maps as you can"), **Adaptation** ("With time, you will develop a personal Mind Mapping style. Once you have created hundreds of Mind Maps, you will be ready to experiment by adapting the Mind Map form"). Source: [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf). (Verified here as Acceptance/Application/Adaptation; the Mind Map Book's own "Accept, Apply, Adapt" wording is secondary.)
- Buzan's training organisation presently teaches "Five Laws of Mind Mapping—Branches, Keywords, Colour, Pictures, and Structure" and a licensed-instructor course. — search summary of [tonybuzan.com course pages](https://tonybuzan.com/about/mind-mapping/) (not fetched in full; secondary). The ten-Law list (MMM18) and the five-Law marketing list are different groupings.

### Inferences
- The two stated primary reasons behind most rules are (a) memory/association ("hooks", "ripple", "bounce"), and (b) visual impact/clarity "to the brain". Very few reasons are evidence-based; they are assertions in Buzan's voice.
- For requirements: one keyword per branch is not purely a stylistic preference but a thinking-process rule ("forced to deliberate which word"). An AI generator that writes whole phrases would defeat the stated purpose. Branch length equal to word length is a layout algorithm requirement (branch drawn under the word, not a box around it).

### Gaps
- *The Mind Map Book* (1993/1996) Laws wording is not verified: its archive.org copies are lending-restricted. "Straight lines are boring to your brain" and "a picture is worth a thousand words" as Buzan quotes were not found in UYH84 or MMM18 (extract). MMM18 Chapter 1 (seven steps, Radiant Thinking definition) is missing from the local PDF.

## 2. Mind Map Mastery (2018): what is NOT a mind map; digital and AI

### Takeaway
MMM18 ch. 3 names five look-alikes (spider, pyramid, concept map, fishbone, sunburst) and gives a nine-question test for "a true Mind Map". Ch. 6 says software that "breaks most of the Laws" produces concept maps, praises iMindMap, and says he has "yet to meet a robot that can create a Mind Map independently from human input".

### Cited Findings (all from [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf), ch. 3 "What Is Not a Mind Map?")
- Spider diagrams: "They often have a highly structured layout, with legs projecting from a central idea. However, unlike Mind Maps, they don't always use colour and very rarely use images. A spider diagram's legs are usually linear and spindly, rather than organic and varying in thickness." He says "My heart sinks whenever I come across ... the mistaken belief that Mind Maps and spider diagrams are the same thing."
- Pyramid diagrams: core concept at top; "the tendency is for the eye to scan the page from top to bottom in a one-directional, rigid, linear manner, rather than ranging freely".
- Concept maps: "words and phrases ... placed in boxes or circles", "connected in a downward-branching hierarchical structure", labelled arrows can be "a phrase rather than a single word, so loses the impact that a single keyword has"; "colours and images are not a feature"; they "often have a pedagogical function ... to be learned by rote, whereas Mind Maps can be used ... for brainstorming and devising new strategies".
- Fishbone (Ishikawa, 1960s): "linear, monochrome" and about cause and effect. Sunburst: "The charts do not use imagery. While perhaps useful for summarizing data, they can be hard to read and are not a great tool for inspired creative thinking."
- Historical claims he rejects: Porphyry's arbor has "no central image ... The thinking here is not radiant"; Leonardo "did not use colour schematically in his diagrams"; Newton's diagrams "were monochromatic" and "tree growing upward rather than expanding radiantly". "To confuse centuries-old diagrams with modern Mind Maps is a little like mistaking a penny farthing bicycle for a supercharged motorcycle!" He does credit Darwin among those "who used diagrams to develop their ideas" and says Leonardo's words-and-images notes "helped shape the early stages of my research".
- Test for "a true Mind Map": "Is there a clear central concept? Is there a clear central image depicting that concept? Does the diagram radiate from the centre? Is there always one word per branch? Are there any images? Is colour used throughout? Is the Mind Map clear? Is the Mind Map natural and organic in appearance? Is the Mind Map highly visually appealing? If the answer to any of these questions is 'no', you are not looking at a true Mind Map."
- "A Mind Map's use of colour, imagery and visual connections is informal, organic – and essential to its proper functioning." "there is no formal iconography, nor a rigorous sense in which a Mind Map can be 'right or wrong'."
- Logic claim: "in a Mind Map things only connect to other elements through the power of association, meaning there is a natural, causal link between them." Mind maps "do not generally relate to steps, systems or processes".
- Failure-mode "imagine losing the Laws" list: no central image, no colour, only straight lines, no images, "more than one word per branch ... how clumsy it would be", unequal branch/word length.
- Chapter 4 (solution finding) also states that many who report trouble "are not in fact working with Mind Maps at all, but are making spider diagrams" and offers an 8-point troubleshooting checklist (unlined A4 horizontal, central image in three colours, eye-catching, main branches different colours, one word per branch, healthy numbers of sub-branches, images throughout).
- Mind Map vs concept map in practice: Buzan's own contributor Marek Kasperski writes of making "very small concept maps to large ones" while following the Laws (ch. 2 box), showing that Buzan's publisher uses the terms loosely.

### Cited Findings: digital and AI (MMM18 ch. 6 "The Future of Mind Mapping"; ch. 5 memory section)
- "over a decade since I teamed up with Chris Griffiths to create the first effective digital Mind Mapping software: iMindMap" ... "Together, we came up with software that reflects the brain's organic nature ... while obeying the Laws of Mind Mapping." iMindMap "used by over one million people".
- "Unfortunately, many of the other apps and software packages that claim to be Mind Mapping tools break most of the Laws. In these instances, the software often generates concept maps in which, for example, the use of colour is optional and there are many 'central' ideas; or a number of words or phrases are erroneously crammed into boxes and placed along the branches ... branches are not always connected, differentiated or ordered hierarchically." He proposes checking "the matrix of the Mind Mapping Laws" against "the mini matrix of software programs".
- Stated benefits of computer maps: editable; backed up; "clear graphics ... easy to read"; linkable to other multimedia; "post-process analysis"; "simple to generate using the supplied drawing tools and icons"; easy to share and annotate.
- Personal preference: "This is one reason why I personally prefer hand-drawn Mind Maps to digital versions, useful though those can be" (memory section of ch. 5; rationale: you must "create your own Mind Map rather than rely on one that has been made by somebody else" because associations are personal, citing Collins and Quillian 1969).
- AI: "we have yet to develop AI that can itself Mind Map by using association and imagination in meaningful ways"; "Rather than create new robotic laws, I would like to see the development of AI that is capable of consciously mastering the Laws of Mind Mapping"; "I sincerely believe that the ability to make a Mind Map would act as the ultimate proof of intelligence in a robotic being." He cites a 2017 Rutgers computational-art study and Gary Marcus (NYT, 29 July 2017) on AI limits.
- 3D: Marek Kasperski proposes 3D mind maps: "a central theme being like holding somebody's brain"; Buzan: "We are now in the process of refining 3D iMindMap software".

### Inferences
- Buzan gives an unusually direct requirement test for software (the Laws matrix; nine-question true-mind-map test). A product claiming "Buzan-style" maps could use those nine questions as acceptance criteria. Concept-map-like features (boxed phrases, optional colour, multiple centres) are exactly what he calls non-mind-maps.
- His 2018 AI statement is a position, not a ruling: he wants AI that masters the Laws. This supports AI generation constrained by the Laws, while his hand-drawn-preference passage suggests value in letting the user redraw or edit rather than only consuming generated maps.

### Gaps
- Nothing from 2018 onward on generative AI (he died in 2019). Ch. 1 is missing from the local PDF.

## 3. Radiant Thinking, imagination and association

### Takeaway
The Radiant Thinking definition proper (MMM18 ch. 1, "page 33") is not in the local PDF. What can be quoted: he treats association and imagination as "twin faculties" of an innate "Human Language", and uses "radiant" as the opposite of top-down, one-directional diagrams.

### Cited Findings
- "every human – you and me included – is fluent in the twin faculties of association and imagination from birth" and ch. 1 "explained the concept of Radiant Thinking and introduced you to the Human Language". — [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf), ch. 2 "A Creativity Workout" box. Also "the twin forces of imagination and association" (ch. 5/6).
- "a Mind Map relies on the power of Radiant Thinking rather than on deduction and reduction. As an open-minded thinking tool, a Mind Map charts the processes of the brain in action instead of fixating on conclusions and outcomes." — MMM18 ch. 2 "A Mind Map for All Ages".
- "By encouraging radiant thinking, we can make the best use of our creative abilities in an easy, natural way" and "The mind really works in multiple thoughts and directions at the same time" — Buzan interview with Stuart Crainer and Des Dearlove, [management-issues.com, 4 December 2007](https://management-issues.com/interviews/4668/tony-buzan-on-mind-mapping/).
- He adds that mind maps are "analytical", and describes radiating "like a starburst" vs trees that grow upward (ch. 3). He links thinking to neurons "with tentacles radiating out from the cell's centre" (Preface). Pass one's secondary quote ("intended to reflect the structure and processes of our brains") is from the Tornhill review, not Buzan.
- Association as the basis for memory: Collins and Quillian (1969) "found that our semantic memory ... is organized like a library of sorts, with interrelated categories or nodes", "everybody has their own spider's web of associations: for example, 'bird' is connected to 'flight' is connected to 'sky'." — MMM18 ch. 5, Key Application 6 (Memory); his reference list cites Collins and Quillian, "Retrieval Time From Semantic Memory", *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior* 8, 1969.
- Early memory principle (UYH84 p. 65, ch. 3 "Memory"): connecting images must be one or many of "exaggerated ... absurd ... sexual ... vulgar ... sensual ... moving ... coloured ... imaginative ... pure ..." (list continues past the lines I read). Memory is "primarily an associative and linking process which depends in large part on key words and key concepts properly imagined" (p. 66, end of Memory chapter). This is the 1984 precursor of SMASHIN' SCOPE; the acronym itself does not occur in UYH84. — [UYH84 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934504509-6qustd.pdf)
- "memory works by association" is stated at UYH84 p. 83 ("We know that memory works by association").

### Inferences
- Product design can use "imagination" (generated art, absurd/humorous images, 3D/dimension) and "association" (arrows, codes, cross-links, colour codes) as the two pillars; both are stated by Buzan as the "twin" engines.

### Gaps
- The exact Radiant Thinking definition sentence from the Mind Map Book / MMM18 ch. 1 is not verified. SMASHIN' SCOPE expansion not verified in primary text (see section 5).

## 4. Hand-drawn vs computer; iMindMap; Ayoa transition

### Takeaway
Buzan's public stance: software is fine if it reproduces the organic hand-drawn feel and obeys the Laws; he personally preferred hand-drawn. iMindMap dates to 2006-2007 (sources differ). Ayoa launched 2019; the Ayoa site states iMindMap would be supported to the end of 2022.

### Cited Findings
- Buzan, e-mail interview with Chuck Frey (undated on the page): the aim was to "design a perfect marriage between the computer and the infinitely associative organic nature of natural human thought"; "iMindMap is the first software in history to accomplish this goal"; "I believe that the team who accomplished this will go down in history alongside Gutenberg". — [Mind Mapping Software Blog, "Exclusive interview with Tony Buzan"](https://mindmappingsoftwareblog.com/?p=1029) (fetched; the page contained no direct Buzan quote comparing software with hand drawing).

## 5. Memory: review schedule, SMASHIN' SCOPE, memory championships

### Takeaway
UYH84 gives the review schedule exactly: first review about 10 minutes after a one-hour learning period (10 minutes long), next about one day later (2-4 minutes), about a week (2 minutes), about one month; "After this time the knowledge will be lodged in Long Term Memory." There is no six-month step in that text; pass-one's "three to six months" is secondary and does not match this edition.

### Cited Findings
- UYH84 pp. 57-60, ch. 3 "Memory" (sections "Memory - review"): "within 24 hours of a one-hour learning period at least 80 per cent of detailed information is lost." "the first review should take place about 10 minutes after a one-hour learning period and should itself take 10 minutes. This will keep the recall high for approximately one day, when the next review should take place, this time for a period of 2 to 4 minutes. After this, recall will probably be retained for approximately a week, when another 2 minute review can be completed followed by a further review after about one month." Also: recall "initially rises, and only then declines" after a learning period (the brain needs "a few minutes ... to let it 'sink-in'"); each review "just before recall is about to drop"; later reviews are done "without referring to final notes, jot down on a piece of paper everything that can be recalled", then checked; "Both notes and jot-downs should be in the form of Mind Maps as explained on pages 112-114." The snowball analogy is the stated benefit. Source: [UYH84 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934504509-6qustd.pdf).
- BOST (Buzan Organic Study Method), UYH84 ch. 5 (p. 117 onward): sections Introduction, Preparation (Time, Amount, Knowledge, Questions) and Application (Overview, Preview, Inview, Review, Noting, Continuing review). Quotes: the order "is by no means essential and can be changed, subtracted from and added to as the study texts warrant." Noting has two forms: "Notes made on the text itself" and "A growing mind map"; straight marginal lines mark important material, curved or wavy lines "to indicate unclear or difficult material" (fig. 54). Mind-mapping advice: "start with a central image that captures the essence of that which you are studying"; "Ideally the bulk of Mind Map noting should take place during the latter stages of study"; "keep enormous 'Central' Mind Maps which summarise and overview the main branches". Also "In most cases it will be found that not much more than 70 per cent of that initially considered relevant will finally be used", and "celebrate!" at completion. Source: [UYH84 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934504509-6qustd.pdf), pp. 117-148.
- World Memory Championships: "In 1991, Ray Keene and I co-founded the World Memory Championships, an event that is separate from the World Mind Mapping Championships ... ten different disciplines". The MMM18 resources list: "Founded by Tony Buzan and Raymond Keene OBE in 1991" (World Memory Sports Council); "World Mind Mapping Council ... Founded by Phil Chambers and Tony Buzan". Dominic O'Brien's record: 2,808 cards on 1 May 2002. Phil Chambers: "World Mind Mapping Champion, the Chief Arbiter of the World Memory Sports Council". Liu Yan won gold at the 2016 World Mind Mapping Championships. — [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf), ch. 5 and Resources. Corroboration: "The championship has taken place annually since 1991, with the exception of 1992" and "originated by Tony Buzan and co founded by Tony Buzan and Ray Keene" — [Wikipedia: World Memory Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Memory_Championships) (pointer only; WMSC founding year not stated there).
- World Mind Mapping Championships criteria: marked on "20 different criteria, such as the impact of the imagery, the use of humour (for instance, through puns), the attractiveness of the Mind Map and its originality, and whether it obeys the fundamental principles of Mind Mapping"; tasks include mapping a lecture or set text. — MMM18 Preface/"Going Forward" (local PDF). Held in the UK, Singapore, China and other countries.
- Dominic O'Brien (Foreword to MMM18): dyslexic as a child; Mind Mapping "helps me stay on track"; "grateful to Tony for inventing the Mind Map".

### Inferences
- A review-reminder feature is supportable on Buzan's text as: 10 min, 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, with recall-from-memory (blank-page map redraw) on later reviews. "Six months" should be dropped or labelled secondary.
- The WMC and WMMC scoring criteria give a ready-made rubric for AI-generated map quality: imagery impact, humour/puns, attractiveness, originality, adherence to principles.

### Gaps
- SMASHIN' SCOPE expansion: still not verified from a book. The only primary memory-image list found is UYH84's 1984 list above (exaggerated/absurd/sexual/vulgar/sensual/moving/coloured/imaginative/pure...), a precursor. Pass-one's secondary expansion stands (Synaesthesia/Sensuality, Movement, Association, Sexuality, Humour, Imagination, Numbers, Symbolism, Colour, Order/Sequence, Positive, Exaggeration; [Art of Memory wiki](https://artofmemory.com/wiki/SMASHIN_SCOPE/) was 403 earlier).

## 7. Other practitioners, criticism, trademark

### Takeaway
Jamie Nast is the clearest sourced dissenter on the one-word rule; Margulies "breaks some traditional rules" (secondary reader reviews); trademark criticism is thin and registry-specific. No source retrieved for Vanda North, Chris Griffiths, Gelb or Wycoff views on relaxing the rules.

### Cited Findings
- Jamie Nast, "Mind Mapping or Idea Mapping?" (Idea Mapping blog, 16 May 2009): "Although there is some value in the laws when applied appropriately, some people feel their creativity is restricted by them." Clients "nearly abandoned the tool because of this (especially by the one-word-per-line law)." "When and where the laws should be applied should be determined by the purpose for which the map is being created." She teaches the laws "in the form of guidelines". She calls one-word-per-line "the second broken mind mapping law" (per fetch summary). — [Idea Mapping blog](https://ideamapping.blogspot.com/2009/05/mind-mapping-or-idea-mapping.html)
- Phil Chambers (a Buzan associate; World Mind Mapping Champion): "At the time they were little more than spider diagrams with lots of colour but lacking structure and including phrases." (describing his school-era maps, MMM18 ch. 5), i.e. he treats phrases as a defect. — [MMM18 local PDF](file:///Users/parth/.claude/projects/-Users-parth-projects-general-mind-maps/e978aa9d-83bf-4c61-9197-231fbb959d27/tool-results/webfetch-1794934529988-lt08q4.pdf)
- Marek Kasperski (ch. 2 box) notes making "very small concept maps to large ones" while following the Laws, which blurs Buzan's concept map distinction; it is a contributor view reprinted by Buzan, not a rule change.
- Buzan's own 1984 text says "key word or phrase" (UYH84 pp. 82-83) while insisting "one word per line" for the map (p. 92): the strict one-word rule is in the map layout, not necessarily the concept of a key. Production-style advice "one word or phrase per branch" appears in a secondary article ([Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/2020/5/11/mind-map-suggestions-for-audio-professionals), search summary only).
- Wycoff (1991, Berkley Books) summarised Buzan's guidelines as "A central focus or graphic representation of the problem is placed in the center of a page; ideas are allowed to flow freely" (cited secondhand). Margulies, *Mapping Inner Space*: reader reviews say she "breaks some traditional 'Mindmapping' rules ... which she now calls 'Mindscaping'". Publication dates conflict (1991 Zephyr Press vs 2002 per Crown House). — search summaries only: [Crown House PDF](https://www.crownhouse.co.uk/assets/look-inside/9781904424475.pdf), [AbeBooks listing](https://www.abebooks.com/9780913705568/Mapping-Space-Learning-Teaching-Mind-091370556X/plp).
- Trademark: Wikipedia says the Buzan Organisation holds "Mind Map" trademarks for self-improvement courses in the UK, USA and Germany (pass-one) and a US application serial 79023104 (filed 2005, "MIND MAPS", classes 9, 41, 16) is listed as dead on an aggregator — [markinton.com](https://markinton.com/trademark/mind-maps-79023104) (search summary; official USPTO TSDR not checked). Wikipedia talk-page editors argue the term is generic ([Talk:Mind map/Archives/2018](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Talk:Mind_map/Archives/2018)), volunteer opinion only.
- Pass-one criticism of science (Foer, Tornhill, Serig) stands.

### Inferences
- Branding risk: describing a product as "Mind Map" may touch Buzan-related marks; use "mind mapping" generically only after a registry check (UK IPO, USPTO, DPMA, EUIPO). Not legal advice.
- A configurable "strict Buzan" vs "relaxed" mode is justified by Nast's critique and Buzan's own Adaptation stage.

### Gaps
- No usable sources found for Vanda North's, Chris Griffiths's, Michael Gelb's, Joyce Wycoff's or Nancy Margulies's own wording; Phil Chambers's learning-tech.co.uk site was not reached. No peer-reviewed criticism of the one-word rule found. Current registry status of "Mind Map" marks not verified.

## 8. Consolidated "Buzan rule set" for product requirements

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blank, unlined, large paper in landscape | Landscape is "easier to absorb at a glance" and allows non-linear scan; lines/decoration "detract from your creative process" | MMM18 ch. 2, Law 1, p. 60 | V |
| 2 | Central image representing the subject, at least three colours, with dimension | "A dynamic central image will automatically focus the eye and the brain, triggering numerous associations" | MMM18 ch. 2, Law 2; ch. 4 checklist | V |
