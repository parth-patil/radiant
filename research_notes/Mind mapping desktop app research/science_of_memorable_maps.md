# Science of memorable maps: evidence for mind maps, concept maps and visual notes (as of Nov 2026)

Research-process note: this run was limited to about 24 tool calls. Search snippets were used and few full texts were read (PMC and Springer pages blocked fetches). Numbers below come from abstracts or secondary summaries and are labelled as such. Topics where I did not retrieve a source are in the Gaps subsections. They are not asserted as findings.

Evidence grades used: STRONG = meta-analysis or multiply replicated; MODERATE = several studies or one review; WEAK = single study or abstract only; FOLKLORE = no support found.

## 1. Direct evidence on mind maps and concept maps

### Takeaway
Concept mapping has meta-analytic support (moderate-to-large effects, bigger when learners construct the map). Branded "Buzan-style" mind mapping has a thin and mixed evidence base: one 2002 study with a motivation caveat, a null replication in 2010, and a 2023 meta-analysis (21 studies) whose effect size I could not retrieve. Most mind-map studies compare against weak baselines such as ordinary note-taking or lecture.

### Cited Findings
- Nesbit & Adesope (2006, Review of Educational Research): 55 studies, 67 effect sizes, N=5,818. One summary gives g=0.82 for constructing concept maps and 0.37 for studying provided maps. The effect varied from small to large depending on how maps were used and on the comparison treatment. [SFU abstract](https://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/articles/nesbitadesope2006.htm), [Schroeder et al. summary](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17789). Grade: STRONG.
- Schroeder, Nesbit, Anguiano & Adesope (2018, Educational Psychology Review 30:431–455): 142 independent effect sizes, n=11,814. Overall g=0.58. Creating concept maps g=0.72 versus studying concept maps g=0.43. [SFU Summit](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17789), [TUM Clearinghouse summary](https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/CHU_KR-19_ENG_Schroeder-2_2017.pdf). Grade: STRONG. Caveat: concept maps (labelled propositions, usually hierarchical) are not identical to Buzan mind maps (radial, keyword, colour, images).
- Farrand, Hussain & Hennessy (2002, Medical Education): 50 second/third-year medical students. Mind-map recall of factual material improved relative to baseline at immediate test. It was robust at one week for the mind-map group only. Motivation was lower in the mind-map group. If motivation had been equal, the estimated improvement would be 15% (95% CI 3% to 27%). So 15% is a motivation-adjusted estimate, not a raw difference. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12028392/). Grade: WEAK (single small study; I found no "10%" figure in any source).
- D'Antoni et al. (2010, BMC Medical Education): no significant differences between mind-map and standard note-taking groups on pre/post quizzes. The follow-up did not find an increase in short-term recall or critical thinking. [Paper](https://www.biomedcentral.com/1472-6920/10/61/), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2949690/). Sample size and exact statistics not retrieved. Grade: WEAK-MODERATE negative.
- Wickramasinghe et al. (2007, South East Asian Journal of Medical Education 1(1):30-32), "Effectiveness of mind maps as a learning tool for medical students." Bibliographic details are confirmed, but I could not retrieve its results. [Author publication list](https://www.res.cmb.ac.lk/medicine/indika-karunathilake/pubs/effectiveness-of-mind-maps-as-a-learning-tool-for-medical-students/). D'Antoni describes it as comparing a mind-map group with a self-selected study group.
- Shi, Yang, Dou & Zeng (2023, Asia Pacific Education Review 24(3):303–317), "Effects of mind mapping-based instruction on student cognitive learning outcomes: a meta-analysis." 21 studies. Mind-mapping instruction gave better cognitive outcomes than traditional teaching. Younger students and STEM subjects benefited most. I could not retrieve the pooled effect size or CI. [AMiner record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/628d1aab5aee126c0f386f37/effects-of-mind-mapping-based-instruction-on-student-cognitive-learning-outcomes-a), [DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/s12564-022-09746-9). Note that it is not in Educational Research Review. Grade: MODERATE, comparison is "traditional teaching".
- Liu et al. 2014 meta-analysis: I could not locate this paper, so its effect size is unverified. Do not cite it.
- Recent related reviews: Faraji et al. (2025, BMC Medical Education), a meta-analysis of concept mapping in nursing education, reports the intervention group improving more than controls. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40437435/). Romanko (UBC thesis 2016): concept mapping and critical thinking in nursing, no statistically significant support. [UBC](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/24/items/1.0228162). A "concept mapping in STEM 2004-2023" meta-analysis exists, but no numbers were retrieved. [Record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-9aa8a715236d48788de4becaeefa849c/Description).
- A claim that mind maps raised children's word recall by up to 32% over lists traces to a vendor blog citing Toi (2009). I could not verify it. [Source](https://mind-map.com/?p=636). Grade: WEAK/unverified.
- A Substack post quoting g of about 1.5 for mind mapping cites no primary source and should be ignored. [Source](https://kwikbrain.substack.com/p/the-thinking-method-historys-most).

### Inferences
- Design implication: build the product around concept-map-like structure (labelled links where relations matter) and not only radial keywords. The strongest evidence is for constructing and for propositional links.
- Design implication: do not market or specify the app with "mind maps improve memory by X%". Defensible claims are "construct-it-yourself maps help; studying someone else's map helps moderately".
- Motivation and novelty are plausible confounds. Farrand's group reported lower motivation, so a delightful visual tool could add real benefit by raising engagement (inference, not tested).

### Gaps
- Pooled effect and moderators for Shi 2023; Liu 2014 existence; Wickramasinghe 2007 results; D'Antoni 2010 N. Check the primary PDFs.
- No source found for training-time or novelty-effect moderators specific to mind maps.

## 2. Self-generated vs provided maps (matters for AI-generated maps)

### Takeaway
Constructing the map beats studying a provided one (g=0.72 vs 0.43 in the 2018 meta-analysis). Generative learning strategies, including learner-made drawings, outlines and graphic organisers, show moderate effects. An AI-generated map should be treated as scaffold material that the learner completes, edits or rebuilds, not as a finished study artefact.

### Cited Findings
- Creating g=0.72 versus studying g=0.43. [Schroeder et al. 2018](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17789). STRONG.
- Fiorella & Mayer (2015), as cited in a 2024 Frontiers paper: drawing beat controls in 26 of 28 studies, median d=0.40; summarising median d=0.50. [Frontiers 2024](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1452385/pdf). MODERATE (secondary citation).
- Learner-generated outlining: g=0.59 memory, 0.59 comprehension (2023 meta-analysis). Learner-generated graphic organisers: g=0.59 memory, 0.63 comprehension (2025 follow-up). Highlighting: 0.36 memory, 0.20 comprehension. All reported through a search summary of Ponce, Mayer & Méndez's work. [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10067-7). MODERATE.
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 14 studies (16 comparisons) found that drawing beats reading alone, but added drawing support (prompts, scaffolds) was not reliably related to outcomes. [Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10648-025-10067-7).
- Chang, Sung & Chen (2002) compared map correction (a map about 40% incomplete), scaffold fading and map generation. They argued that building maps promotes deeper processing but can overload learners, and proposed faded scaffolds as the compromise. [Chang et al. PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2002-chang.pdf). WEAK-MODERATE.
- Ruiz-Primo et al. (2001): fill-in-the-nodes and fill-in-the-lines formats are not equivalent, and construct-a-map better captured differences in learners' knowledge structure. This is an assessment study, not a learning-gain study. [SERC](https://serc.carleton.edu/resources/14980.html).
- A conference-abstract comparison (AERA 2026) found that restating expert maps in the learner's own words gave better transfer than filling in blanks or constructing from a concept list. Treat as preliminary. [AERA abstract](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2282457/). WEAK.

### Inferences
- Design implication: AI generates a draft skeleton (structure only, possibly partial); the learner must edit, reorder, relabel or complete it. Offer a "study mode" that blanks 30-40% of nodes or links (Chang's incomplete-map format), with fading over repeated reviews.
- Design implication: add "explain this branch in your own words" prompts for AI-provided branches (self-explanation, per the AERA abstract).
- Design implication: do not make learners pay the cost of generating all images. Let AI propose images but require the learner to choose, edit or replace them, so there is some generation.
- No direct study of AI-generated maps and learning was found.

### Gaps
- Fiorella & Mayer's original source for generative-learning theory was not read directly. I found no controlled study on AI-generated concept maps and retention.

## 3. Retrieval practice vs concept mapping

### Takeaway
Karpicke & Blunt (2011) showed retrieval practice beating elaborative concept-mapping study, even on a concept-map test. Practice testing and distributed practice are the two techniques Dunlosky et al. rated high utility. A map tool should include retrieval modes.

### Cited Findings
- Karpicke & Blunt (2011, Science 331:772-775), about 200 students across two experiments. Retrieval practice produced more learning than elaborative concept mapping, including on a criterion test requiring concept-map construction. Reported as about 50% better after one week in a secondary summary (not verified in the paper). Students predicted mapping would be better. The authors note concept mapping could itself be used as a retrieval method. [Purdue release](https://www.purdue.edu/newsroom/research/2011/110120KarpickeScience.html), [Stafforini PDF listing](https://stafforini.com/works/karpicke-2011-retrieval-practice-produces/). STRONG for retrieval practice generally (replicated widely, not cited individually here).
- Dunlosky et al. (2013, Psychological Science in the Public Interest): practice testing and distributed practice rated high utility; elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaving moderate; summarisation, highlighting, keyword mnemonic, imagery for text, and rereading low. [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html). STRONG (review).
- Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted & Pashler (2008, Psychological Science 19:1095-1102): over 1,350 participants. The optimal inter-study gap is a proportion of the retention interval, about 20% for tests a few weeks away, falling to about 5% for a one-year delay. Secondary examples: 1 day for a week, 11 days for a month, about 3 weeks for 70 days. [USF repository](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1766/), [York summary](https://yfile.news.yorku.ca/?p=9454). STRONG for spacing; the exact schedule is population- and material-specific.

### Inferences
- Design implication: provide "collapse and recall": hide a branch's children or labels, ask the learner to recall them (free recall first, then reveal), and record correctness.
- Design implication: a spaced-review scheduler per branch. Choose the gap from the learner's target date (about 10-20% of the interval). A simple expanding schedule is a reasonable default.
- Design implication: make retrieval, not rereading, the default way to revisit a finished map. Warn that rereading the map feels productive but is a low-utility technique.
- Retrieval-based concept mapping (map from memory after closing the source) is an inference from Karpicke's own remark, not a tested result in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Follow-up debate papers (for example Lechuga et al. 2015, Blunt & Karpicke 2014) were not retrieved.

## 4. Pictures: picture superiority, dual coding, limits

### Takeaway
Recognition memory for pictures is very large, but picture benefits in learning depend on the picture's function. Decorative additions can hurt, and a conference abstract and a 2012 meta-analysis report a small harm from seductive details.

### Cited Findings
- Standing (1973, Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology 25:207-222): participants viewing up to 10,000 pictures showed near-limitless recognition capacity; about 83% accuracy at 10,000 is from secondary sources. [Standing PDF](https://www.sas.upenn.edu/psych/rust-lab/publications/standing%5F73.pdf). STRONG for recognition; it does not show that pictures help recall of verbal knowledge.
- Rey (2012, Educational Research Review 7(3):216-237): meta-analysis of 39 experimental effects; seductive details hurt, small to medium for retention and medium for transfer. [ERIC listing](https://www.fachportal-paedagogik.de/literatur/vollanzeige.html?FId=eric_ej986386). A conference abstract (50 studies, 177 effects) reports a small negative effect, g=-0.16, mediated by extraneous load. [AERA](https://convention2.allacademic.com/one/aera/aera26/online_program_direct_link/view_paper/2273554/). Counter-evidence: Tislar & Steelman (2021) found no consistent harm in 132 students. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8442593/). MODERATE, partly contested.

### Inferences
- Design implication: each node image should carry meaning (represent the concept) and not merely decorate. The AI image prompt should say "depict the concept itself; no background clutter, no unrelated ornament".
- Design implication: give a "decorative off" toggle and let learners compare. Keep decoration low-contrast when it does not encode meaning.
- Design implication: keep the text label next to the image. Pictures should not replace words for abstract concepts.

### Gaps
- Paivio's dual coding, Carney & Levin (2002) picture functions (decorative, representational, organisational, interpretive, transformational) and the signalling/contiguity principles were not retrieved in this session. They are well known but I have no URL, so I do not assert specific findings. Check Carney & Levin 2002 (Educational Psychology Review) and Mayer's Cambridge Handbook.

## 5. Drawing effect and AI-generated pictures

### Takeaway
Drawing a concept beats writing it down (strong, replicated in the authors' lab), and drawing quality does not seem to matter. There is no evidence I found that merely viewing AI-generated pictures yields the same benefit, so the mechanism (generation and elaboration) points toward letting users draw or sketch.

### Cited Findings
- Wammes, Meade & Fernandes (2016): seven free-recall experiments; drawn words were recalled better than written ones, often more than twice as many; drawing quality did not seem to matter. [Waterloo release](https://uwaterloo.ca/psychology/news/need-remember-something-better-draw-it-study-finds), [UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/a8b52734-18f3-4527-8be2-008a105c85f2). MODERATE-STRONG (same lab, many experiments). Not a meta-analysis; no pooled g found.
- Jonker & Wammes (2019): drawing effect found for both pure and mixed lists. [PDF](https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/jonkerwammes19.pdf).
- Fiorella & Mayer: drawing d about 0.40 median (see section 2).

### Inferences
- Design implication: add a quick sketch mode (finger/pen doodle on a node) alongside AI images, and encourage "doodle first, AI-polish after" so some drawing generation happens.
- Design implication: because quality does not matter, do not push users toward polished art for memory purposes. Polish serves beauty and motivation.
- Whether AI-generated pictures work like drawings is untested in what I found. Treat them as ordinary pictures (recognition benefits, no generation benefit).

### Gaps
- No studies on AI-generated images and recall were found.

## 6. Distinctiveness: von Restorff, bizarreness, emotion, humour

### Takeaway
Distinctiveness helps only relative to context (within a mixed list). A map where every branch is unique in the same way is no more distinctive than a uniform one. The benefit comes from contrast.

### Cited Findings
- McDaniel & Einstein (1986, JEP: LMC 12:54-65): bizarre imagery aided recall when bizarre and common items were mixed within a list, not when varied between lists. [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/bizarre-imagery-as-an-effective-memory-aid-the-importance-of-dist/). McDaniel et al. (1995) found that the mixed-list structure drives the effect. Order-encoding rival accounts exist. [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/download/191922754.pdf), [concept summary](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/bizarreness-effect/). MODERATE, replicable under mixed-list conditions.
- The von Restorff isolation effect is within-subject by definition: the isolate must differ from others in the same list. [Bizarreness summary](https://concepts.dsebastien.net/concept/bizarreness-effect/).

### Inferences
- Design implication: do not give every branch an equally ornate image style. Use a restrained base style and reserve a distinctive treatment (size, colour, style, unusual image) for the few nodes the learner must remember (key facts, exceptions).
- Design implication: the AI prompt should vary imagery within a map (different metaphors per branch) but keep one coherent style family for coherence.
- Bizarre images may also reduce order memory, so keep sequence cues (numbers, arrows) for ordered content.

### Gaps
- Emotional-salience and humour effects: no sources retrieved.

## 7. Spatial memory, method of loci, layout stability

### Takeaway
Method-of-loci training produces large word-list gains in healthy adults (Dresler 2017), but I found no direct experiment showing that stable node positions on a map improve recall. The layout-stability design implication is reasonable but inferred.

### Cited Findings
- Dresler et al. (2017, Neuron): 23 memory athletes vs 23 matched controls; 51 mnemonic-naive men randomised to six weeks of method-of-loci training, working-memory training, or none. Word-list recall rose from about 26-30 words to well beyond that in the loci group, with gains persisting at four months; brain connectivity moved toward the athletes' pattern. All male, single lab, word lists. [Stanford release](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2017/03/memorization-tool-bulks-up-brains-internal-connections.html), [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/03/170308131236.htm). MODERATE (RCT, small, men only).

### Inferences
- Design implication: never re-layout automatically after a user has seen the map. Use incremental layout that only moves new nodes, keep absolute positions on save, and allow "pin".
- Design implication: a large zoomable canvas with distinctive regions (landmarks) is analogous to a memory palace. This is a hypothesis for user testing, not an established result.
- Design implication: store the map as fixed coordinates so that SVG/image re-rendering does not change geometry.

### Gaps
- Spatial contiguity, spatial-position memory for text (for example Rothkopf's work), and semantic zooming (Bederson's Pad++, Furnas) were not retrieved. I have no sourced evidence for overview+detail comprehension benefits.

## 8. Colour

### Takeaway
There is a review literature suggesting colour supports attention and memory, but the effects are mixed and depend on whether colour carries information. I only saw the abstract-level summary.

### Cited Findings
- Dzulkifli & Mustafar (2013, Malaysian Journal of Medical Sciences 20(2):3-9): review of colour in attention, arousal and memory; earlier work found coloured multimedia gave better attention and memory. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3743993). MODERATE-WEAK (narrative review; I read only a summary).

### Inferences
- Design implication: use colour to encode category (branch = hue family), consistently, and not as arbitrary decoration. Keep a colour-blind-safe palette and do not rely on colour alone.

### Gaps
- No quantitative colour-coding effect sizes were retrieved. Signalling principle and Gestalt similarity are unsourced here.

## 9. Cognitive load, chunking, branch counts

### Takeaway
Working-memory capacity is about four chunks (Cowan), which loosely supports keeping each node's visible children small.

### Cited Findings
- Cowan (2001, Behavioral and Brain Sciences 24:87-114): capacity of about four chunks, not Miller's 7±2, tied to the focus of attention; not universally accepted. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/). MODERATE.
- Seductive-detail results in section 4 support the coherence principle indirectly.

### Inferences
- Design implication: default AI generation to roughly 3-5 main branches and about 3-5 children per node, with deeper detail collapsed. This is a heuristic derived from Cowan; there is no direct test on maps in my sources.
- Design implication: collapsing and progressive disclosure fit the segmenting idea, but I did not retrieve the primary source for it.

### Gaps
- Mayer's principles (coherence, signalling, contiguity, segmenting) and Sweller's load theory were not sourced in this session.

## 10. Keywords vs phrases (Buzan's one-word rule)

### Takeaway
I found no evidence for the one-word-per-branch rule. Treat it as folklore.

### Cited Findings
- No source found supporting single keywords over short phrases. Concept-map research favours labelled propositions on links ([Schroeder et al. 2018](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/17789) for the benefit of construction; the proposition format itself is not tested in the retrieved sources).

### Inferences
- Design implication: allow short phrases, and let AI default to terse labels (2-5 words) because of space and load. Do not enforce one word.

### Gaps
- No direct experiment found.

## 11. Curved or organic lines, aesthetics

### Takeaway
There is evidence that people prefer curved objects, but no evidence that curved branches improve memory. Treat this as a preference and motivation lever.

### Cited Findings
- Bar & Neta (2006, Psychological Science 17:645-648): participants preferred curved objects over sharp ones (d=0.94, t(13)=3.53 reported for preference; 140 object pairs and 140 pattern pairs); exceptions exist. A 2007 fMRI follow-up found more amygdala activity for sharp objects. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16913943/). A later critique questions how well it generalises. [Carbon 2010](https://www.experimental-psychology.de/ccc/docs/pubs/Carbon2010b.pdf). WEAK for learning, MODERATE for preference.

### Inferences
- Design implication: organic branches are justified on preference and engagement grounds. Do not claim a memory benefit. Keep them as a style option, and make sure curves never obscure which node a branch connects.

### Gaps
- Aesthetic-usability effect and processing fluency were not sourced. I found no study linking aesthetics of maps to retention.

## 12. Creativity and brainstorming

### Takeaway
Little rigorous evidence. Claims are mostly from vendors.

### Cited Findings
- A University of Minnesota preliminary study reports that mind maps capture depth and breadth of associations that lists do not show. [UMN](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/mapping-associations-exploring-divergent-thinking-through-mind-ma/). WEAK.
- A study of 39 students found SCAMPER essays rated somewhat more creative than mind-map essays, not significantly. [SAGE (proxy mirror)](https://https-sage-cnpereading-com-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/doi/10.1177/00336882211066624). WEAK.

### Inferences
- Design implication: position the app as a thinking and structuring tool. Do not promise creativity gains.

### Gaps
- No controlled map-versus-list idea-count study found.

## 13. Known negatives and limits (summary)

### Takeaway
Mixed results are the norm when comparisons are active or motivation is controlled.

### Cited Findings
- D'Antoni 2010: no advantage over note-taking ([BMC](https://www.biomedcentral.com/1472-6920/10/61/)).
- Farrand 2002: lower motivation for mind maps ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12028392/)).
- Karpicke & Blunt 2011: retrieval beat mapping ([Purdue](https://www.purdue.edu/newsroom/research/2011/110120KarpickeScience.html)).
- Chang et al.: constructing maps can overload ([PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2002-chang.pdf)).
- Rey 2012: seductive details harm ([ERIC](https://www.fachportal-paedagogik.de/literatur/vollanzeige.html?FId=eric_ej986386)).
- Nursing critical thinking: no significant effect ([UBC thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/24/items/1.0228162)).
- Dunlosky 2013 rates imagery use and summarisation low ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)).

### Inferences
- Design implication: pair every map-building feature with a retrieval or spacing feature, since mapping alone is not the high-utility technique.

### Gaps
- No systematic list of null studies was compiled.
